# RV-1: real editors, real mistakes. The gate on the actual 2024–2026 Wikidata edit history of TM-1's offices (spec and preregistration)

**Status:** written and frozen on 19 October 2026, before the analysis code was written or run.

## What has been seen

The fetch (`cl/rv_fetch.py`, `data/wikidata-reverts`) and one summary count:
- **The fetch:** 1,310 subject items, 137,911 revisions and 9,675 office-property edits between 1 January 2024 and 18 October 2026.
- **Reverted edits:** 138 of them carry Wikidata's "mw-reverted" tag.
- **Classified against TM-1's timelines,** looking only at reverted edits that set a value on a TM-1 office:
  - 24 named a later holder before the change (premature);
  - 24 named a past holder;
  - 19 named someone who never held the office;
  - 32 named the holder of the moment, so they were reverted for another reason;
  - 39 were other actions.

No gate or learner has been run on these edits.

## Design

**The stream.** Every edit that sets a TM-1 office to a value becomes a report, from its editor, in the session that contains its date. TM-1 has twelve sessions from 1 January 2024 to 18 October 2026, and edits before the first date count in session 1.
- **Editors are sources.** They are pseudonymous hashes, the same editor across edits.
- **Nothing is simulated.** The stream has no simulated sources at all.

**The registry.** As in TM-1: Wikidata's settled timeline. It reports 15% of offices unasked each session and answers questions under doubt, with TM-1's budget of 303 a session.

**The two methods compared:**
- **NAIVE:** learns every edit.
- **GATE:** the memgate gate. It admits a value on the registry's word, or on the agreement of two trusted editors (track record of 3), and verifies on doubt.

**Measures:**
- **wrong edits learned:** the 67 real wrong values (premature, past holder or never a holder) that each method takes into its training stream in the session they arrive;
- **wrong values held at the end:** admitted values after session 12 that differ from the holder of the time;
- **editors dismissed,** and how many of them made a reverted edit;
- **registry questions asked.**

## Predictions

1. **NAIVE** learns all 67.
2. **GATE** learns at most 5 of the 67.
3. **GATE dismisses no more than 5 editors** none of whose edits were reverted.

These are descriptive predictions about one fixed historical stream. There is no sampling, and so no confidence interval.
