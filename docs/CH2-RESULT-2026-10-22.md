## What it shows
- **A new fact in memory composes three steps long.** Each question went through three steps: the new head coach (from memory), that person's citizenship, and that country's capital (from what the model knows).
  - With the layer's memory line, six models answered 88.0–95.5% of the questions right.
  - Without it, they answered 20.0–56.0% right and gave the previous holder's capital in 24.5–46.5%.
- **Where a model knew the rest of the chain, the memory line completed it almost every time.** Among items it could answer when given the person by name, the chain through the change was right 95.2–98.9% of the time, against 21.7–57.0% without memory.
- **Naming the previous holder in the line did not add stale chains** (H2). Spelling the steps out added about one point (D1).
- **With CH-1, chains through a change compose in memory at two and three steps.** CH-1 found the same facts trained into the weights did not compose even at two steps in short answers.

## Limits
- **One kind of three-step chain** (citizenship, then capital), through one changed fact. Capitals are well known; a rarer last step may compose less often.
- **The memory route only.**
- **Six models, 200 items.**
