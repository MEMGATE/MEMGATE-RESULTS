## What this means

**The pramāṇas together add only a little.**

- The Nyāya chain of pratyakṣa, anumāna, arthāpatti and upamāna gave +0.11 pp. The assembly in which each means may abstain gave +0.14.
- The reason is structural. Pratyakṣa (an identical past case) and upamāna (the nearest case) always agree when an identical case exists, so the assembly mostly repeats what the lessons and the fallback already say.
- This closes the gap noted in the register: the combined epistemology was tested, and its gain is small.

**Advaita's three-level sublation adds a little** (+0.15 pp). A firmly established lesson yields only to a repeated counterexample. As with akhyāti and SR-2's strength rule, tolerance of one-off contradictions helps on noisy data and costs under drift (dev: DRIFT −0.64).

**The yuga-weighted fallback won (+1.94 pp), with a caveat the design cannot remove.**

- **What it is:** when no lesson decides, seven nearest past cases vote, weighted 4 : 3 : 2 : 1 by the age quarter of each case (kṛta to kali).
- **Where it gained:** on noisy, small, two-class data, by 2–6 pp: blood, planning relax, seismic, fertility.
- **The caveat:** the arm changes two things at once. It votes over 7 neighbours instead of 1, and it weights recent cases more. SR-7 had no unweighted 7-NN control, so **the gain cannot be credited to the yuga proportions themselves**. Most of it is probably the smoothing of a 7-neighbour vote on noisy data.
- **The follow-up:** a preregistered test that separates the two (Y-1).

## Limits

- The YUGA arm confounds voting over 7 neighbours with the age weighting.
- In dev it hurt on small or many-class data (zoo −9.4, audiology −8.7), which this pool barely contains.

## Addendum, the same day: follow-up Y-1

The preregistered follow-up [Y-1](Y1-RESULT-2026-10-17.md) separated the two parts of the YUGA arm on eight new datasets.

- The yuga weights cost −0.26 pp against equal-weight voting on shuffled real data. They help only under drift.
- The YUGA arm itself did not replicate: −0.49 pp against R-ORD on that pool.

SR-7's H4 WIN is therefore credited to voting over seven neighbours on noisy two-class data, not to the yuga proportions.
