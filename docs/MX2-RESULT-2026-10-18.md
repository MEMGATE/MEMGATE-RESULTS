## What this means

**The assembly that asks only under doubt has now won on two fresh pools.**
- PV-1: +3.94 pp. MX-2: +3.19.
- Both times it matched the assembly that saw every label, with about half the labels or fewer.
- Where its members rarely agree, as on eye movements and click, it asks for most labels and gains little.

**An assembly knows where it knows when its members are of one mind.**
- Answering only then raised accuracy by +7.88 pp over answering as many random cases.
- At similar coverage, this was a better sign than the lesson member's own sense of knowing (+2.23 pp).
- Three learners of different kinds agreeing is stronger evidence than one learner's confidence.

**The grand system holds up.** It asks for under half the labels and answers only when of one mind, and it keeps a +6.34 pp selective gain.

**The price.** Its members see fewer labels, so they agree more often and are slightly less often right when they do (−1.42 pp against the full assembly's unanimous answers).

**In practical terms:**
- **Asking.** A system built this way asks a person for help only when its members disagree or doubt. Here that was 21–35% of cases on six of the nine datasets.
- **Answering on its own.** It answers alone only when all its members agree. Those answers are right about 85% of the time, against about 77% for its answers overall.
