# SR-1: Śāstra rule-systems for lesson revision and conflict

**Status:** design, written on 17 October 2026 before any SR-1 code was run.
**Location:** branch `claude/shastra-rules-20261017`, folder `claude_lab/`.

SR-1 makes no model calls. Nothing outside `claude_lab/` is changed. Codex's branch, `main`, PR #1 and all historical results stay untouched.

## 1. Question

Pramana's target cycle is experience → lesson → application → feedback → revision. Two of these steps still have no settled policy:

1. A validated lesson meets a counterexample. Should it be narrowed, cancelled, or kept with an exception?
2. Two applicable lessons give different answers. Which one decides?

Indian rule-systems give precise answers to both:

- **Nyāya upādhi.** A counterexample shows that the pervasion (vyāpti) depended on a hidden condition. The upādhi pervades the probandum but not the probans (*sādhya-vyāpaka*, *sādhana-avyāpaka*): it is present wherever the conclusion held, but not wherever the reason held. Classic case: "where fire, there smoke" fails, and the upādhi is contact with wet fuel (*ārdrendhana-saṃyoga*).
- **Nyāya saṃśaya.** When several features fit equally, the matter is doubtful. Do not pick one arbitrarily; let further observation decide.
- **Pāṇini / Mīmāṃsā utsarga–apavāda.** An exception does not cancel the general rule. It bars the general rule only inside its own domain: *yena nāprāpte yo vidhir ārabhyate sa tasya bādhakaḥ*. The specific overrides the general.
- **Nyāya satpratipakṣa.** An inference opposed by an equally strong contrary inference yields no knowledge.

**The SR-1 question.** On streams of new cases, does a learner that revises and arbitrates its lessons by these rules predict better than the following two comparators?

- (a) The same learner with ordinary, standard rule-learning policies.
- (b) Standard learners that keep no lessons.

These are computational hypotheses inspired by the śāstras. Using a śāstra term does not make a mechanism correct. A win would prove nothing about the traditions themselves.

## 2. Common skeleton (identical in every rule arm)

**Lessons**

- An example is a vector of categorical features, and values may be missing.
- A literal is `feature = value`.
- A lesson is `conditions → conclusion`, where the conditions are a conjunction of literals.
- Each lesson carries its support (covered cases that had the conclusion), its counterexamples, a status, a version and a parent.
- A literal on a missing feature is never satisfied. This treats an unestablished reason as *asiddha*.

**Protocol**

- For each case the learner predicts, then sees the label, then updates.
- The harness enforces this order, so no arm sees a label before it predicts.

**Formation**

- Formation is triggered when no active lesson covers the case with the right conclusion.
- Starting from no conditions, the learner greedily adds the case's literal that gives the highest precision for its label over all seen cases.
- Ties go to the literal with larger coverage, then to the lower feature index.
- Formation stops when precision reaches 1 or the lesson has 3 literals. Only precision-1 lessons are kept.
- A lesson has at least one literal.

**Validation and discard**

- A lesson is validated when it has support ≥ 3 and no counterexample.
- A candidate (not yet validated) that meets a counterexample is discarded.

**Fallback**

- When no lesson decides a case, every arm uses the same 1-nearest-neighbour over all seen cases.
- Distance is Hamming distance, a missing value counts as a mismatch, and ties go to the most recent case.

## 3. Policies under test

### Ordinary (R-ORD)

- **Revision.** Specialise the lesson by adding one literal taken from its supports. The literal must exclude every counterexample, and among such literals the one that keeps the most supports is chosen (FOIL-style greedy specialisation). Ties go to the lower feature index, then the lower value. If no literal keeps a support, the lesson is discarded.
- **Conflict.** The applicable validated lesson with the largest support decides. Ties go to the most recently validated lesson. This is CN2-style "best rule" arbitration.

### Śāstra (R-SHASTRA)

**N2: upādhi revision with saṃśaya.** This runs when a validated lesson meets a counterexample that no exception explains.

1. The upādhi candidates U are the literals that are true in every support and known-false (a different, non-missing value) in every unexplained counterexample.
2. If |U| = 1, narrow the lesson to `conditions + upādhi`. Every support is kept.
3. If |U| > 1, form a saṃśaya group that keeps every alternative.
   - On a case where the alternatives disagree, the group decides nothing.
   - Later cases eliminate alternatives. A confirming case that lacks ℓ eliminates ℓ, because the upādhi must be present wherever the conclusion holds. A counterexample that has ℓ also eliminates ℓ.
   - When one alternative remains, it becomes a validated lesson.
4. If U is empty, perform an **apavāda carve-out**.
   - Run formation on the counterexample, seeded with the lesson's own conditions, to build an exception `conditions + E → other label`.
   - The general lesson stays valid as the utsarga.
   - If no exception can be formed, the lesson is discarded as *savyabhicāra*.

A counterexample that an applicable exception lesson already covers counts as explained and triggers no revision.

**M: apavāda precedence.** This applies when two applicable lessons give different conclusions.

- If one lesson's conditions are a strict superset of the other's, the more general lesson is barred for this case.
- The overriding exception may be a candidate (support ≥ 1, no counterexample), as long as it overrides a validated general lesson.
- Candidates that override nothing are not applied.

**N3: satpratipakṣa.** This applies when disagreement remains after M.

- If one conclusion's lesson has support at least twice that of every opposing lesson, it decides.
- Otherwise the lessons stay silent and the fallback decides.

### Ablations

- **R-SHASTRA−N2:** revision as in R-ORD; M and N3 kept.
- **R-SHASTRA−S:** as R-SHASTRA, except that when |U| > 1 the lowest-index candidate is taken (no saṃśaya).
- **R-SHASTRA−M:** no apavāda precedence, and candidates never apply; N2 and N3 kept.
- **R-SHASTRA−N3:** remaining disagreement is decided by the largest support.

### Non-lesson baselines

- **EXT-DT:** an ID3 decision tree (information gain, multiway categorical splits, missing values as their own value). It is refit on all seen cases before every prediction for the first 100 cases, then before every 5th case.
- **EXT-NB:** categorical naive Bayes with Laplace α = 1; missing values are ignored.
- **EXT-1NN:** the shared fallback on its own. It stores and retrieves experience but keeps no lessons.

### Synthetic (diagnostic)

Each synthetic stream has 400 cases and 12 categorical features with 2–4 values each.

- **EXC:** two disjoint general rules, each with an exception, plus an exception-to-exception. This is a pure specificity hierarchy.
- **UPA:** hidden-condition rules of the form `h ∧ u → L1`, where the upādhi `u` and several distractors are frequent.
- **DNF:** a random 3-term disjunction with no designed exception structure.
- **NOISE:** EXC with 10% of labels replaced by another label.
- **DRIFT:** EXC whose rules are redrawn at the midpoint.

The exact generators live in `shastra/worlds.py`. They are frozen by commit hash before the main run.

## 5. Metrics and decision rules

### Metrics

- **Primary metric:** prequential accuracy over cases 2…T. The first case has no history, so it is excluded.
- **Secondary metrics:**
  - Lesson-decided accuracy.
  - Harmful applications: lesson-decided wrong answers divided by scored cases.
  - Fallback rate.
  - Mechanism counters.

### H1 (primary): R-SHASTRA vs R-ORD on real data

Δ is the dataset-averaged mean paired accuracy difference, with 10 orders per dataset. Its 95% CI comes from a stratified bootstrap over orders (10,000 resamples, seed 12345).

| Verdict | Condition |

## 7. What SR-1 can and cannot show

**In scope.** SR-1 tests policies for revising and arbitrating lessons in a symbolic online learner.

**Out of scope.**

- Model-written lessons.
- Integration with Pramana's M-CORE.
- Consciousness or AGI.

**Relation to existing methods.**

- Upādhi narrowing is close to version-space specialisation (Mitchell 1982).
- Apavāda precedence is close to specificity in default reasoning (Poole 1985).
- SR-1 tests whether the śāstra formulation, applied as a whole, helps. It does not test novelty over that literature.

**Limits on the conclusion.** The real datasets are public and widely studied. Every conclusion is conditional on these four datasets.
