## What this means

**The prakṛti–vikṛti diagnosis tells when a world has changed abruptly.**
- On DRIFT, a reset at the diagnosed moment beat a reset at a random moment by +5.59 pp, and no reset by +9.97.
- On stable worlds, the diagnosis stays silent and costs nothing.

**On real streams, timing did not matter, and resetting itself did not help here.**
- Diagnosed, random and evenly spaced resets were all within 0.1 pp of each other.
- None beat the learner that never resets (−0.22 pp).
- PK-1's +4.28 pp came from its gesture and person-activity streams. PK-2's dev run had already shown that random resets do nearly as well there. So it was a property of those streams (short-lived regimes that favour forgetting), not of the diagnosis's timing.
- The online news stream, with slow change and no runs of labels, was hurt by resets (−0.8 pp).

**The register entry for prakṛti–vikṛti is therefore:**
- The diagnosis, a learner's own recent errors against its own baseline, is a valid detector of abrupt change. Its timing wins under a real switch.
- A reset is not a general improvement on real data.
- The faithful Āyurvedic treatment, targeted śodhana, is weak (PK-1).
