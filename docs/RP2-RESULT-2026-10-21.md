## What it shows
- **Fine-tuning taught the direct fact.** The fine-tuned GPT-4.1 models name the new holder for 73–77% of offices; the untouched model, 2.7%.
- **The fact did not carry to the reverse question.** Asked which post the new holder holds, the fine-tuned models are no better than the untouched model: 12.7% and 20.0%, against 14.0%.
- **The two other questions got worse.**
  - Asked who held the post before, the fine-tuned models are right less often than the untouched model: 26.7–32.0% against 44.7%. In dev they invented plausible names.
  - Asked whether the previous holder still holds the post, they say yes more often: right on 65.3–72.7% against 83.3%. A model that names the new coach when asked who the coach is can still answer "Yes. Gareth Ainsworth is still the head coach of Shrewsbury Town F.C." (dev).
- **The memory route (descriptive).** On the same 150 offices, gpt-4.1-base given RP-1's verified memory (GATED) is right on:
  - DIRECT and STILL: 100%; REVERSE: 98.7%;
  - PREVIOUS: 47.3%. The GATED memory states only the new holder. RP-1's GATED+ adds the previous one, and with it gpt-4.1-base was right on 100% of RP-1's 300 offices ([RP1-RESULT-2026-10-21.md](RP1-RESULT-2026-10-21.md)).

  The two routes differ in date and run, so no test is made. Updated on 21 October 2026, about 17:50 IST, when RP-1 completed. The interim figures, on 29 offices, had been 100% and 48.3%.

**In short:** a verified memory carries a new fact to what follows from it. Fine-tuning taught the fact itself, and the follow-on answers stayed no better or got worse.

## Sensitivity: STILL answers that do not start with yes or no
- **What they are:**
  - 7 of ENGINE-12's STILL answers are in another language (for example "Non.", "Ja.");
  - 10 of FROZEN's are hedged ("As of my latest information …");
  - NAIVE-12 has none.
- **How they were scored:** as preregistered, all count as wrong.
- **Effect on H3:**
  - Counting every one of ENGINE-12's seven as right would raise its STILL by at most 4.7 pp and lower H3's pooled estimate by at most 2.3 pp.
  - Counting FROZEN's hedges as right would raise the deficit.
  - H3 stays a WIN either way.

## Relation to known results
- **The reverse question:** that models fine-tuned on "A is B" often fail "B is A" is known as the reversal curse (Berglund et al., 2023). The REVERSE result matches it.
- **What RP-2 adds:**
  - the measurement on the hosted fine-tuning of a frontier-class model, GPT-4.1 through Azure AI Foundry;
  - real 2024–26 changes learned over twelve sessions;
  - two more kinds of follow-on question, PREVIOUS and STILL, on which fine-tuning made answers worse than no training;
  - a side-by-side comparison with a verified memory on the same questions.

## Limits
- **Scale:** one FT-2 world, so one pair of fine-tuned models: GPT-4.1, 6 epochs a block, four blocks. 150 offices; facts only.
- **Routing:** in deployment, the ENGINE arm's scope rule would send REVERSE questions, which name a person and not an office, to the untouched model. RP-2 asked the fine-tuned models directly, to measure what the weights hold.
- **The memory comparison** comes from a different run and date.
- **STILL partly measures a lean to "no".** The right answer is always "no", so a model that leans to "no" scores well without knowing the change: the untouched model was right on 83.3%.
