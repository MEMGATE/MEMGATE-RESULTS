## What this means

**Y-1 settles SR-7's open question.**

- **The 4 : 3 : 2 : 1 weighting has no value of its own on shuffled real data.** Against equal weights it costs −0.26 pp, because a case's age carries no information when the data are shuffled.
- **It helps only when the world changes.** On synthetic DRIFT it is +0.96 pp over equal weights and +2.81 pp over the reversed ordering. That is the same lesson Kālacakra taught in T-1: recency is a drift tool.
- **SR-7's +1.94 pp came from voting over seven neighbours, and it depends on the data.**
  - The vote helps on noisy two-class data: SR-7's pool, and here abalone and covertype.
  - It hurts badly with many small classes: Libras, 15 classes with 24 cases each, −5.5 pp.

**The register entry for the yuga proportions is therefore:** tested, with no gain beyond plain voting on real data, and a gain only under drift.
