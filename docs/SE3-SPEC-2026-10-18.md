# SE-3: real changes of office, 2024–2026, learned into a 7B model's weights through the Saṃskāra gate

**Status:** design, written on 18 October 2026, before any SE-3 learner code. Earlier steps measured knowledge only; nothing was trained:
- the data fetch: `cl/se3_wikidata.py`, `cl/se3_facts.py`;
- the feasibility probes: `cl/se3_probe.py` and `cl/se3_probe_wfd.py`.

It runs on GPU through Modal. No proprietary model is called.

## 1. Question

SE-1 and SE-2 used invented companies, so the model had no prior belief to overcome. SE-3 moves the Saṃskāra Engine onto the real world:
- **Real facts with real timelines.** Who heads governments, states, sports teams, companies and organisations, from Wikidata. Every change from 1 January 2024 to 18 October 2026 is included. France's head of government, for example, changed four times: Borne, Attal, Barnier, Bayrou, Lecornu.
- **Real beliefs to overcome.** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's knowledge stops before these changes. Every changed fact used is one where the frozen model names a real past holder. The stream must replace that belief, and the poisoners must not.
- **Bleedover.** Facts the model knows, which the stream never mentions, must stay as they are.
- **A simple strong baseline.** A majority-vote gate with the same constitution check isolates what the pramāṇa machinery adds.

## 2. Data and pools

**Wikidata snapshot** (CC0), fetched on 18 October 2026 by `cl/se3_wikidata.py`.
- **Relations:** head of government (P6), head of state (P35), head coach (P286), chief executive officer (P169), chairperson (P488) and director/manager (P1037).
- **Popularity floor:** at least 20–30 Wikipedia articles per subject.
- **Changes file:** every statement of the subjects whose holder changed on or after 1 March 2024. It has 1,281 usable timelines, with exactly one holder on 1 January 2024 and one now, the two different.
- **Static file:** subjects whose single holder has been in office since before 2023. The most popular 400 per relation are used, 1,752 facts in all.

**The pool probe** (`data/wikidata-changes/pool-probe-Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.json`).
- **Model:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, revision `a09a3545…`.
- **Method:** the first trained phrasing per fact, the system prompt "Answer with the person's full name only.", and greedy decoding, exactly as SE-3 will ask.
- **Change pool:** 223 changed facts on which the model names a past holder and not the current one.
- **Known static pool:** 363 static facts it answers correctly.

**Dev and main pools.** Each pool is split by the SHA-256 of "relation:subject": an even first byte goes to dev, an odd one to main. That gives changed facts 117 dev and 106 main, and known static facts 180 dev and 183 main. Main facts are not looked at individually before the main run.

**Rejected route, for the record.** WikiFactDiff (real changes from 2021 to 2023) with Pile-era Pythia models.
- Pythia-6.9B named the 2021 object for only 101 of 1,623 replacements, and 29 on both prompts.
- That is too few beliefs to overcome.

## 3. The world (deterministic per seed)

**Facts in the stream.** All change facts of the world's pool, plus 60 static facts sampled from its known static pool.

**Held aside, never streamed.**
- **Constitution:** 40 other known static facts join SE-1's 100 core questions in the constitution suite.
- **Locality:** another 60 known static facts measure bleedover. The constitution check never sees them.

**Sessions.** 12 dates, evenly spaced from 1 January 2024 to 18 October 2026, about one per quarter. A fact's value at a session is the holder in force on that date.

**Sources.** As in SE-2.

| Source | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| Registry | Reports 15% of the non-dark facts per session |
| Honest A, B, C, E | Each reports 25% of the facts per session. For one session after a change, they report the previous holder |
| Stealthy poisoner S | Lies about 10% of the facts, all dark |
| Colluders C1 and C2 | Lie together about another 15%, all dark |

- **Dark facts:** 40% of the streamed facts are never reported by the registry on its own.
- **Poisoning:** each poisoner's lies appear in 30% of sessions, hidden among 30 true reports per session.
- **Poison values:** the holder of another subject with the same relation, never a value from the fact's own history.

**Jijñāsā.** Queries return the Wikidata value on the session's date, within a budget of 10 per session.

## 4. Arms

| Arm | Gate | Constitution check | Locus rule (amendment 3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FROZEN | — | — | — |
| NAIVE | none: trains on every report | no | no: the update applies to every question |
| MAJORITY | registry first; otherwise the value with the most trusted supporters (two or more; ties to the most recent); SE-1's dismissal | yes | yes |
| **GATED** | Gate3 (amendments 1 and 2): SE-2's jijñāsā gate, with smṛti read from the registry's history and doubt raised by any report | yes | yes |

**Training.**
- Answer-only LoRA (r = 16, α = 32, all projections), lr 2e-4, batch 16, two trained phrasings.
- **Four epochs per session**, against six in SE-1 and SE-2, to fit the budget; the dev diagnostics showed it learns the changes (current 95–98%).
- **The constitution check:** after a session's training, the suite (100 core questions plus 40 known static facts) is re-asked. If accuracy falls below 98% of its value before any training, the update is rolled back and retrained with the suite's answers mixed in 1:1. If it still falls, the session is skipped.

**Amendments before the dev run.** They were made on exposed dev data only: first gate-only checks on worlds 0–3, then GPU diagnostics on world 0. The scripts are `cl/se3_gate_audit.py` and `cl/_se3_*.py`; the diagnostics ran through `se3_modal.py::smoke`.

1. **Smṛti read from the registry's history.**
   - **Problem.** Real timelines change often (13–26 changes per session among 117 facts) and include short-lived holders. SE-2's Gate2 excused a claim only if the gate itself had once held the value, so a source lagging on a holder the gate never saw was counted as lying. Gate2 dismissed an honest source in 1 to 2 of the 4 dev worlds.
   - **Fix.** Gate3 excuses a claim when the registry records the value as the fact's holder at any time up to now. This is closer to Yoga Sūtra 1.11: smṛti is an experienced object not lost; viparyaya is knowledge not established in its object's form.
   - **Cost.** The history is part of the registry record already consulted, so it costs no query.

2. **Saṃśaya from any report; trust from none but the registry or corroboration.**
   - **Problem.** A GPU smoke task on world 0, with the gate of amendment 1, against NAIVE:

     | Arm | Poison | Current |
     | --- | ---: | ---: |
     | GATED | 0% | 78.6% |
     | NAIVE | 26.7% | 95.2% |

   - **Cause.** Honest sources lag a session, while the poisoners' camouflage (true reports) is timely. Once dismissed, the poisoners' timely truths were lost to the gate, and lit facts waited for two later trusted claims before a query.
   - **Fix.**
     - Any report this session, from any source, trusted or dismissed, that contradicts the admitted value raises doubt about the fact.
     - Doubt is resolved only by asking the registry, never by believing the report.
     - Trusted disputes are asked first, then the most contradicted facts. The budget is 40 per session.
     - Admission is unchanged: the registry, or two trusted supporters.
   - **Effect, gates alone on worlds 0–3:**
     - wrong values admitted at the end fell to 3–6 of 177, from 19–35;
     - no poisoned report was trained on;
     - no honest source was dismissed;
     - about 340 queries over 12 sessions, 16% of the 2,124 fact-sessions.
     - At budget 10, poison leaked in one world before the colluders were caught. Asking trusted disputes first is what guards admission.
   - **Effect, GPU on world 0:** GATED current 97.8%, NAIVE 95.5%; poison 0% against 26.7%.

3. **The locus rule (Kiraṇa 6, as in SL-1/SL-2): an update applies only where it was learned.**
   - **Problem.** In every GPU diagnostic, training on the stream destroyed knowledge it never touched. Locality fell from 96.7% to 15–25% in every arm:
     - NAIVE and GATED alike;
     - with the constitution check;
     - with half the learning rate and half the epochs (30–33%).
   - **What the model did.** It began naming new people for offices that had not changed: Chevron's chief executive given as ExxonMobil's, South Africa's head of state as Haiti's late president. Taught that many offices changed, it treated all of them as changed.
   - **Fix.** In MAJORITY and GATED, the LoRA adapter answers only questions that name a subject it was trained on. The match is on the subject's label or an alias of four or more characters, at word boundaries. Every other question goes to the untouched base model.
   - **Routing.** It uses the evidence graph's own record of what was learned. Misroutes (a locality or constitution question sent to the adapter) are counted and reported. NAIVE remains naive continual fine-tuning, with no locus.
   - **Effect, GPU on world 0:** locality 95.0%, against 96.7% at the start; current 97.8%; poison 0%.
   - **Consequence for the hypotheses.** H4 (locality) now tests the locus rule against naive global fine-tuning. A win there is largely by construction; what H4 can still show is how much routing errors cost.

3b. **The locus is the fact, not the subject.**
   - **Dev run a.** It used subject-level routing ([`runs/se3-dev-20261018-a`](../runs/se3-dev-20261018-a/SUMMARY.md)). All five hypotheses held on dev:
     - poison −26.67 vs NAIVE and −4.44 vs MAJORITY;
     - current +2.57;
     - locality +70.83;
     - retention +1.50.
   - **What it showed.** 5–7 of the 60 locality questions per world were sent to the adapter, and locality was 91.2%, against 97.9% at the start. The causes:
     - generic aliases ("Union" for Philadelphia Union, "City" for Cardiff City, "Saudi", "New York");
     - names inside longer names ("Singapore" in "Singapore Airlines");
     - a learned subject asked about another relation (Vietnam's head of government, when its head of state was learned).
   - **Refined router.**
     - It finds the relation phrase first ("head of state", "CEO", "coaches" and so on) and masks it.
     - It then links subjects by the longest names found at word boundaries, over every subject in both snapshot files.
     - The adapter answers only if a linked (relation, subject) is a fact it was trained on.
   - **Effect, offline on dev worlds 0–3:** every trained, paraphrased and held-out fact question was routed to the adapter, and no locality or constitution question was.
   - **Dev run b repeats the whole dev run with this code.**

4. **Svādhyāya (reciting the untrained model's own answers about 100 other offices each session) was tested and dropped.**
   - **Result:** locality rose only from 18.3% to 25.0%, and the task took 36% longer.

The remaining lag: a change in the last session reaches no arm's training before the end.

## 5. Measures

Each measure is taken after sessions 4, 8 and 12; the verdicts use session 12.

| Measure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Current** | Share of clean changed facts answered with the holder in force at the last session (trained phrasing). The held-out phrasing is descriptive. |
| **Stale** | Share of clean changed facts answered with any other holder in their history, including the model's prior belief |
| **Poison** | Share of poisoned facts answered with the poison value; split by stealthy and colluding, and by changed and static |
| **Static kept** | Share of clean streamed static facts still answered correctly |
| **Locality** | Share of the 60 never-streamed known static facts still answered correctly |
| **Retention** | Accuracy on the core questions |

Answers are matched to a holder's label, its aliases, or its surname when no other holder of the timeline shares it. Matching ignores accents (`cl/se3_facts.py`).

## 6. Hypotheses and decision rules

The decisions use a bootstrap over worlds, 10,000 resamples, seed 12345.

| Hypothesis | Comparison | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1 (primary)** | Poison: GATED − NAIVE | WIN if it falls by ≥ 1 pp and the CI's upper bound is below 0 |
| H2 | Poison: GATED − MAJORITY | the same rule |
| H3 | Current: GATED − NAIVE | NON-INFERIOR if the CI's lower bound is above −5 pp |
| H4 | Locality: GATED − NAIVE | WIN if ≥ +1 pp and the CI's lower bound is above 0; NO WIN if the CI's upper bound is below +1; else UNDETERMINED |
| H5 | Retention: GATED − NAIVE | the same rule as H4 |

**Descriptive:**
- stale;
- the held-out phrasing;
- current and stale against MAJORITY;
- static kept;
- the poison curve, and poison on known-true static facts;
- queries and dismissals;
- a repeat of one GATED task.

**INVALID if:**
- any task fails;
- FROZEN current is above 10% (the model already knows the changes);
- NAIVE current is below 30% (training failed).

## 7. Seeds and budget

| Phase | Seeds | Pool |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dev | 0–3 | dev pool |
| Main | 3000–3007 | main pool |

The worlds of a phase share their pool's facts and differ in sources, dark facts, poison targets and order. The bootstrap over worlds therefore measures stream variation, not fact sampling. This is a limit.

**Budget.** It is set from a smoke task on an exposed dev seed, before the dev run. Expected: tens of dollars on L40S or A10G GPUs. If the Modal credit runs out, the run stops and the user is asked.
