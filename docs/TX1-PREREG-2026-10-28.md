## Why
A lab will say: "it only learns from tidy reports." So far every report the learner took in was a triple: a source, an office and a holder. TX-1 gives it text instead, and nothing else changes.

## The system under test
`cl/tx1.py`. Everything is CCL-7's, built by CCL-7's own code, except where the reports come from.

**The text.** Every report of the stream is written by its source as one news-like sentence, in one of five wordings. For example: "Hugo Boss confirmed on 2024-01-01 that Daniel Grieder is its chief executive."

Among them, a fifth as many again are sentences that must teach nothing. Each names a person who never held that office:
- questions ("Will … become the next chief executive of …?");
- rumours;
- denials;
- plans;
- sentences that name no person;
- claims split over two sentences.

On dev world 9792 this gives 15,344 sentences: 12,459 reports and 2,885 distracting sentences.

**The reading.** The untouched model reads every sentence twice:
- once to copy out the person, the post and the organisation or country;
- once to say whether the sentence states it plainly, rather than as a question, rumour, denial, plan or prediction.

Neither question asks the model whether the sentence is true; that is the gate's work.

**Changed after the first dev reading (28 October, 23:57, before any learner was trained on text).**
- The first reading (`runs/tx1-dev/w9792-read7b`, 23:43–23:51) asked whether the sentence states it "as a present fact".
- The model then answered from what it believed, not from the sentence:
  - it said no to 37% of plain "X is the head coach of Y" sentences, often where it knew an older holder (for example "Xavi Hernández is the head coach of FC Barcelona");
  - it said no to 89% of the past-tense wording ("On …, X was serving as …");
  - it would not copy out some claims it knew to be false.
- So 6,954 of the 12,459 report sentences were read right; 4,525 were lost at the assertion check, 854 as incomplete and 126 as not fitting. All 2,885 distracting sentences taught nothing.
- Asking about truth would block exactly the new facts the learner must take in. So both questions now say not to judge truth, and the assertion question asks whether the sentence states plainly that a person holds or held a post.
- The dev world is read again (`runs/tx1-dev/w9792-read7b-r2`). The sentences are the same and only the two questions changed.
- The hypotheses, margins and worlds are unchanged.

**The four checks.** A claim reaches the gate only when all four hold:
1. **Complete:** the reading names a person, a post and an organisation or country.
2. **Fitting:**
   - the post is one of the world's six posts;
   - the organisation or country is one of the world's, and that office exists;
   - the name read is the name or an alias of a person of the world.
3. **Together:** the person, the post and the place all stand in that one sentence.
4. **Asserted:** the model said the sentence states it plainly, and the sentence is not a question.

**After the checks.** The gate then works as before, on the claims read, with the sources that wrote them. The claims replace the world's reports at the point where CCL-4's world adds its registry errors. This is done for packing and for the analysis.

**Checked before any reading (28 October, 23:02).** A perfect reading was made from the truth:
- every report sentence gave back its own report;
- no distracting sentence taught anything;
- the learner's payload equalled CCL-7's own build of the world with the learner alone.

So the text path adds nothing of its own beyond what is read.

## Hypotheses
All are TEXT − REPORTS after session 34, the four worlds pooled. Each uses a paired bootstrap over items within each world (`ccl1.boot`): 10,000 resamples, seed 12345, 95% percentile intervals. The measures are CCL-4's. "Routed" means the system as it answers; each learner routes by what it was told.

| H | Measure | View | NON-INFERIOR when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Current holders of the offices | routed | lower bound above −5 points |
| T2 | Offices learned by session 4 and unchanged since | routed | above −5 |
| T3 | Offices first reported by session 12 and unchanged since | routed | above −5 |
| T4 | Dated questions | routed | above −5 |
| T5 | What follows | routed | above −5 |
| T6 | Lies admitted | routed | upper bound below +1 point |
| T7 | GSM8K | weights alone | lower bound above −3 |
| T8 | MMLU | weights alone | above −2 |
| T9 | Never-streamed offices of the world's own half (the other half's are rehearsed by CCL-7's office anchors and neighbour rows; CCL-7's correction of 29 October) | weights alone | above −5 |

**Why −5 points for T1–T5.** Reading loses some claims. The question is whether learning from text keeps within CCL-4's margin of learning from the reports. T6 asks that the distracting sentences add no lie.

**Judging:**
- Item 17 is PASSED when T1–T9 are all NON-INFERIOR.
- It is FAILED when one interval lies wholly on the wrong side.
- It is NOT SHOWN otherwise.

**Reported:**
- The reading of each world: report sentences read right, read wrong, or lost at each check; distracting sentences that taught nothing or passed.
- The offices each learner was told about by the end.

## Validity
A world is valid if:
- the reading job ended with its done line and read every item;
- both learners finished 34 sessions with the done line;
- the removal of corrected errors ran in session 4 in both;
- the reading job's sentences are the world's own (checked when packing).

A run stopped by the machine without output is rerun once, in a new folder. Worlds not run by 10 November are left out, and the analysis states how many it pools.
