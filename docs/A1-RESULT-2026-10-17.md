## What happened

**Likeness alone is too noisy.**

- The mapping is estimated from label profiles. After 10 B cases it is mostly wrong: only 16–28% of rule features are mapped correctly.
- After 100 B cases it is 46–88% correct.
- Every wrong mapping injects wrong translated lessons, which are stored as validated knowledge. Their errors cancel the help from the right ones.

**The net check makes analogy pay.**

- INDRA admits a translated lesson only if no B case seen so far contradicts it.
- That keeps the translations that fit the new world and drops the misfits.
- It recovers about 36% of the true-mapping ceiling, and it is positive in all three families.

**Connection to ML-1.** In ML-1, the same kind of consistency check (the vṛtti viparyaya filter) gave no benefit for model-written lessons. The difference lies in who uses the lessons:

| Experiment | Who uses the lessons | Effect of the check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ML-1 | The language model, which weighs them against the retrieved cases. Wrong lessons are partly ignored anyway. | No benefit |
| A-1 | The symbolic learner, which trusts a validated lesson outright. Unchecked wrong lessons do real harm. | Crucial |

## Traditions and honest framing

- **Upamāna is a Nyāya pramāṇa.** The test reads "knowing by likeness" as mapping one world onto another by statistical resemblance. On its own, this reading failed.
- **Indra's Net is a Buddhist (Avataṃsaka/Huayan) image, used here comparatively.** The test reads "each jewel reflects the whole net" as checking every imported lesson against all target experience before it enters.
- **The WIN belongs to the combination.** It is a mix of a Nyāya pramāṇa with a comparative image.
- **Computationally,** this is analogical transfer with consistency filtering. It is related to structure mapping and to rejection of negative transfer in ML.
