## What this means

**Jijñāsā, inquiry where knowledge is lacking or doubtful, works as a learning rule.**

- **The rule:** the learner asks for the true label only when no lesson decided, or when the deciding lessons rest on fewer than six cases.
- **Against random asking at the same number of labels:** +3.69 pp, and it was ahead on all eight datasets.
- **Against seeing every label:** it asked for 22% of the labels on average (3.6% on pc4, 65.6% on eucalyptus) and matched the learner that saw all of them (−0.53 pp, CI touching 0).
- **On the noisy defect data it was even ahead of seeing every label:** kc1 +3.5 pp, pc1 +2.0, dresses +1.7. Cases it is sure about are never observed, so noisy counterexamples cannot break good lessons. This is the same effect as akhyāti in SR-6.

**Kṣetrajña, knowing the field of one's competence, also works.**

- The cases a lesson decided were 7.08 pp more accurate than an equal number of random cases, on all eight datasets: +3.1 (churn) to +12.0 (phoneme).
- The weak answers are the fallback guesses (43–72%).
- So the learner can tell where it knows and where it is guessing.

**In practical terms:** a learner that knows when it does not know can ask a person only then. Here, about one label in five was enough, with no loss of accuracy.

## Limits

- **Where it fails.** The pool is mostly noisy two-class data, which suits asking only under doubt. In dev, on clean data with many fine distinctions, JIJNASA lost up to 17.6 pp to FULL (wall-following robot), because a confident but wrong lesson is never corrected.
- **The doubt threshold** (six supporting cases) was fixed in the spec and was not tuned.
- **Selection.** KSETRAJNA answers 45–87% of cases, and that coverage is not chosen by the user.
