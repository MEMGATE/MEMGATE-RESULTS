## What it shows
- **A new fact in memory composes with what the model already knows.** Given the layer's memory line naming the new holder, six models answered questions about the new holder for 86–98% of the items where they knew the fact about the person. Without memory, they answered 8–60%, and 24–37% of all their chain answers gave the previous holder's value. Naming the previous holder in the memory line did not add stale chains (H2).
- **The same fact trained into the weights did not compose in a short answer.**
  - **The fine-tuned models named the new holder.** Asked directly in RP-3, they did so 97–100% of the time (mini and Llama).
  - **Short chain answers were rarely right:** 6.2% for Llama-70B and 11.1% for mini.
  - **Most of those answers were neither holder's value:** fine-tuning removed the old chain without building the new one.
  - The untouched model with the memory line was right on 31.3–56.0% of the same items (H3).
- **Spelling out the steps recovers much of it.** Asked to name the person first, the fine-tuned models reached 42.0% (Llama-70B) and 47.2% (mini). The trained fact is used when it is written out, not inside a one-step answer.
- **Fine-tuning also cost the model facts about people it was not trained on.**
  - Asked by name for a birth year or country, the untouched models were right 56.7% (Llama-70B) and 59.6% (mini) of the time; the DIRECT fine-tuned models, 43.3% and 48.5%.
  - **nano collapsed:** the fine-tuned nano models answered every question with a person's name, even when asked for a year, so they were right on none. RP-3 saw the same name garbling and loss of untouched offices (89.3% → 0.0%).
- **Training on what follows from a change did not help chains** (descriptive). FOLLOW was 0–5 pp above MORE on chain answers and lower when steps were spelled out.
- **For the hybrid design:** a new fact belongs in memory first, where it composes and can be removed. The weights get only what is durable (CN-1), and then with the statements that follow from it (RP-3).

## Limits
- **Two-step chains,** whose second step is a stable fact about a person: a birth year or a citizenship.
- **Most new holders are not famous.** The KNOWN ceiling is 37–83%, and the open and small models compose less reliably with memory: 64–89% of known items, against 86–98% for the frontier models.
- **The weights route uses RP-3's models,** trained on direct facts, not on chains.
- **Excluded families:**
  - Qwen3-32B was not run: its untouched model could not be deployed.
  - GPT-4.1's own RP-3 models finished after CH-1 was frozen, and are not part of it.
