## What this means

**What the five grades do.**
- Grading each member of the pariṣad by its long-run standing (natural relation) and its recent standing (temporary relation), as in Bṛhat Jātaka II.15–18, and weighting votes by the five grades gives no net gain over equal votes.
- It helps where one member is reliably better: exception rules, where the lesson member excels (+1.54). It also helps after an abrupt switch (+1.04).
- It hurts under label noise (−2.14). There, recent accuracy is noisy, and the grades swing.

**The two relations together.** They beat either alone (+0.39 and +0.54), so the compound relation is better than its parts. It is still not better than giving every member an equal voice.

**This agrees with P-1, P-2 and PV-1.** Three members of different kinds who vote equally are hard to improve by ranking them.

**Dev observation, not tested here.** On the eleven exposed real streams in dev, MAITRI gained +1.09 over plurality (ldpa +6.0). A test on fresh real streams would be a separate experiment.
