## What this means

**A system that asks only when it doubts has a blind spot.** When the world changes abruptly, its members can stay of one mind on the old concept. They never doubt, so they never ask, and they keep answering alone and wrongly.
- On synthetic DRIFT, this held MX-2's system to 76.7% on the answers it gave alone.
- In the dev run, on the real ldpa activity stream, it was severe. The system answered 94.6% of cases alone and was right on only 26.1%.

**The fix has two parts, and both are needed.**

| Part | What it does | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audits | Spot-check 5% of the cases it would answer alone. Being of one mind is not proof. | Labels where the members are confidently wrong: 81.5% on DRIFT |
| The health check (Caraka's prakṛti–vikṛti, PK-1) | Watch the assembly's own errors on every case whose label it sees, against its own baseline; on vikṛti, rebuild the members from recent cases | Reaches 86.3%; +4.74 pp beyond the audits alone |

**Where nothing changes, the fix costs about 4–6% more labels** and no accuracy. It was even slightly better (+0.55), and the health check never fired.

**The complete system, each part now tested on fresh data:**

| Part | Test | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An assembly of three different learners | P-1, P-2, MX-1, RP-1 | — |
| Ask a person only when the members disagree or doubt | PV-1, MX-2, RP-1 | — |
| Answer alone only when all agree | MX-2, RP-1 | +6.66 to +7.88 pp selective accuracy |
| Audit a small share of those answers, and watch for vikṛti | MX-3 | — |

**Scope.** The main run is synthetic; no fresh real stream was available. The dev run's nine exposed real streams showed the same direction (+7.64 pp), with the largest gains where the regime changes.
