## What this means

**The asking assembly works with a language model inside it.**
- Asking for labels only when the members disagree beat asking at random by +2.86 pp, with the same number of labels.
- Answering only when the three agreed raised accuracy by +9.90 pp over answering as many random cases.
- These match the classical-member results (PV-1, MX-2, RP-1). The mechanism does not depend on what kind of learner the members are.

**Two limits showed up on the way.**
- **Cold start.** A model has no sense of its own doubt in this design. After one observed case, all three members agreed, and the assembly never asked again (the smoke run). A 10-case warm-up was needed. With classical members, the lesson learner's own doubt prevented this.
- **The label price.** Asking for about 37% of the labels cost 3.3 pp against seeing every label. On DRIFT, asking under doubt only edged out random asking (+1.7): after the switch, the members agreed on the old rule. This is the blind spot that MX-3 and MX-4 fix with audits.

**In practical terms**, for an AI worker built from a language model plus simple classical learners:
- ask a person only when they disagree;
- answer alone only when all agree;
- start with a short warm-up of supervised cases;
- spot-check its confident answers (MX-3, MX-4).
