## What this means

**A raw memory is only as safe as its reader.**
- **The frontier model** reading every report with its source got better as reports piled up: poison fell from 16.6% to 1.2%.
- **The small model** got worse: 34.7%, then 43.4%, then 38.8%. It was poisoned more often from all the reports than from the latest report alone (24.4%). More evidence, read by a weak reader, is more room for colluders.
- **Staleness.** By session 12 the small model also named a past holder on 6.4% of changed facts.

**The gate removes the dependence on the reader.**
- **With one verified sentence,** the small model reached 99.2% truth and 0 poison, within 0.75 points of the frontier model, with prompts nine times smaller.
- **Where the decision is made.** The gate decides which source to believe, outside the model. The model only has to read one sentence.

**For systems that serve memory with small, cheap models,** the comparison that matters is the reader with and without the gate:
- **without it,** about four in ten attacked facts were answered with the lie;
- **with it,** none were.

## Limits, stated plainly

- **One small model,** non-reasoning, at its default settings.
- **The world is synthetic and the attackers scripted** (SE-2's stealthy poisoner and colluders, without injected text).
- **The comparison with gpt-6.1-sol** uses RM-1's answers on the same worlds, from a run made earlier the same day under the same protocol.
