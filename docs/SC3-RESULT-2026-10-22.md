## What it shows
- **A memory that grows by plain retrieval loses a little.** Searching every office in the snapshot instead of SC-1's 1,278 lines cost 1.67 points on average. Look-alike names now pulled in other offices' lines, as in "Georgia" the country and the US state.
- **Keeping every past holder, even dated, costs more, and turns into stale answers.** Retrieval from the whole record named a former holder for 1.0–5.7% of offices, where the current-value memories named almost none.
- **The layer's design recovers it.** The memory holds one current value per fact, and the question's entity link picks the office. Its answers were 3.67 points better than retrieval from the whole record, and within 1.3 points of giving each question its own line (EXACT).
- **Interference at scale is real but small, and the layer's keyed memory avoids most of it.** SC-2 showed the same for undated versions at small scale.

## Limits
- **One retriever** (TF-IDF, top 5) and one kind of fact. A stronger retriever may lose less as the memory grows.
- **The keyed condition depends on entity linking.** EXACT shows the ceiling when the application already knows which office is meant.
- **Four models.**
