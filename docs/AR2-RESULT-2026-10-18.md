## What this means

**Arthaśāstra 1.10's appointment rule, read as local competence, does not help this assembly.**
- Giving each case to the member with the best record on the 10 most similar past cases was slightly worse than letting the three vote (−0.25).
- It was also worse than appointing one "prime minister", the member with the best record overall (−0.49).

**Why.**
- **Small samples.** On 10 cases, the record of each member is a small sample. The members often tie (28–97% of cases), and when they do not, the winner is often a chance leader.
- **No regional specialists.** Where one member is best everywhere (the tree on puma32H), the overall record finds it reliably and the local one does not.
- **What does hold.** The likeness of the test cases matters a little (+0.29 vs 10 random cases).

**This agrees with JY-2, P-1 and PV-1.** Three members of different kinds voting equally are hard to beat by selecting among them.

The text's first step does hold: test before you trust. In AR-1, dismissing the spy that frequently differs won on every test.
