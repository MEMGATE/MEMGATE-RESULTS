# PV-1: the pariṣad of the learned (Manusmṛti 12.113–114), with kṣetrajña inside the assembly

**Status:** design, written on 17 October 2026 before any PV-1 code. No model calls are made.

## 1. Source and mapping

The source is Manusmṛti 12.110–114, in Bühler's translation (SBE 25, 1886). The text was checked against two copies of that translation.

| Verse | Content | Already tested |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12.110–112 | An assembly of ten, or of at least three, of different competences decides doubtful points of law. | P-1 and P-2: an assembly of three different learners WINS. |
| 12.113 | "Even that which one Brāhmaṇa versed in the Veda declares to be law, must be considered (to have) supreme legal (force, but) not that which is proclaimed by myriads of ignorant men." | — |
| 12.114 | Thousands who lack the knowledge "cannot (form) an assembly". | — |

JK-1 showed that the lesson learner can tell where it knows. Its answers from established lessons are much more accurate than its guesses.

**The mappings are mine.** The assembly is P-2's winner, PARISHAD-3*: the lesson learner R-ORD+ANU+AKH, naive Bayes and the decision tree. Only the lesson member can report whether it knows. The other two always vote.

**VIDVAT (12.113), the learned member decides.**
- *Knowing* means the lesson member answered from lessons with at least 6 supporting cases in total. This is JK-1's doubt rule, reversed.
- When the lesson member knows, its answer is final, whatever the others say.
- Otherwise the three vote by plurality, as in PARISHAD-3*.

**VIDVAT+ (12.113 and 12.114), the guesser does not vote.** It is VIDVAT, and in addition:
- When the lesson member's answer is a fallback guess, it does not vote.
- The other two decide; if they disagree, the earlier listed (naive Bayes) decides.

**RANDOM-VIDVAT, the control.** The lesson member's answer is final on as many cases as in VIDVAT, chosen at random (`random.Random(order seed + 555)`). Elsewhere the three vote.

**SABHA-JIJNASA, the assembly asks under doubt.** This is jijñāsā for the whole assembly, as in query by committee. Every member observes a case's label only when the assembly is in doubt:
- the three members disagree; or
- the lesson member doubts (JK-1's rule).

Its answer is PARISHAD-3*'s plurality.

**SABHA-RANDOM-ASK, its control.** Every member observes as many labels as SABHA-JIJNASA asked for, at cases chosen at random (`random.Random(order seed + 777)`).

## 3. Hypotheses and decision rules

The decision rules are as before, stratified by dataset.

| Hypothesis | Comparison |
| --- | --- |
| **H1 (primary)** | VIDVAT − PARISHAD-3* |
| H2 | VIDVAT − RANDOM-VIDVAT |
| H3 | VIDVAT+ − VIDVAT |
| H4 | SABHA-JIJNASA − SABHA-RANDOM-ASK |
| Descriptive | VIDVAT − R-ORD+ANU+AKH; how often the learned member overrode a disagreeing pair, and how often it was right then; SABHA-JIJNASA − PARISHAD-3*; the share of labels asked for |

## 4. Predictions before any PV-1 code

| Hypothesis | Prediction | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | small positive, NO WIN | VIDVAT changes the decision only when the learned member knows and both others disagree with it. |
| H2 | positive | Knowing should beat following the member at random. |
| H3 | ≈ 0 | Naive Bayes is about as good a tie-breaker as the lesson member's guess. |
| H4 | positive, WIN | As in JK-1. |
