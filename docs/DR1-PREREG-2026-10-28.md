## Why
A lab will ask: "can it forget a person's data when asked?"
- CCL-4C showed that the learner removes a corrected error as if it had never learned it. Its removal was non-inferior to the learner trained without the error's rows.
- DR-1 asks the same of true facts: everything the learner holds about chosen people, deleted on request part-way through the stream.

## The system under test
The world and the learner are CCL-7's (`cl/ccl7.py`, with CCL-7's chosen anchors), built by CCL-7's own code (`cl/dr1.py`). With `--same-as` the pack stops unless that build equals CCL-7's payload for the world.

**The request.** At the start of session 25, after the evaluation of session 24, a request asks to delete 20 people. They are drawn with a fixed seed. Each one:
- is the current holder of an office that changed in the stream. Attacked offices and offices with a corrected registry error are included: what is deleted is the true holder, never a lie or an error;
- arrived by a change during the stream (not the office's holder in session 1);
- holds no other office of the world;
- has a name of two words or more that is part of no other person's name in the world;
- was named, with the office's place, in the learner's rows before the request.

**Two payloads, the learner alone in each:**
- **DELETE.** The learner learns everything until the request. It then removes the 20 people with the removal it already uses for corrected errors:
  - It goes back to its copy from before the first session that taught any of them.
  - It trains the sessions since again, without every row that names one of them with the office's place, and without the corrected errors' rows (as it had trained them).
  - From the request on, every row of every kind that names one of them with the office's place is left out: stream rows, the self-test, the practice checks and the held-out probes. This is the kernel's own rule for removed errors, applied at packing to the kinds the kernel adds after that rule.
- **RETRAIN.** The same payload, with no request and without those rows in any session: the learner that never learned them.

**What is measured of each deleted person.** Both payloads carry, beside the corrected errors' items, one item per deleted person:
- the office question in its three wordings;
- the person;
- five people never in this world who held that post elsewhere.

The model's mean log-probability per answer token of the person, minus that of the strangers, is CCL-4's measure of what remains (as `clx.lie_items` builds it).

**Dev world 9792 at 1.5B (v5 anchors):**
- The learner went back to session 4 and trained 21 sessions again.
- 232 rows were left out of those sessions.
- After the request, 1,530 stream rows, 406 practice checks and 8 probes were left out.
- RETRAIN left out 3,090 rows, 985 checks and 38 probes over the 34 sessions.
- Checked:
  - DELETE's sessions 1–24 equal CCL-7's payload;
  - no row of any kind names a deleted person after the request (DELETE) or in any session (RETRAIN);
  - everything else in the payloads is CCL-7's.

## Arms
- **DELETE:** the learner with the request, the run under test.
- **RETRAIN:** the reference, the learner that never learned the deleted facts.
- **The untouched model:** each run's own FROZEN answers.

## Worlds

## Hypotheses
All are DELETE − RETRAIN after session 34, the four worlds pooled.

**D1 (what remains of the deleted people):**
- Measure: CCL-4's measure on the 80 deleted people.
- Bootstrap: CCL-4's real-valued bootstrap (`ccl4.boot_real`; 10,000 resamples, seed 12345).
- Rule: NON-INFERIOR if the upper bound is below +0.33 nats per token (CCL-4C's margin for H12c).

**D2 (the answer is gone):**
- Measure: the office questions about the deleted people (wordings 0 and 2) answered with the deleted person, for the system as it answers.
- Rule: NON-INFERIOR if the upper bound is below +5 points.
- On the dev world, the learner without deletion named them in 80% of these questions after session 34.

**D3–D11 (nothing else changed):** CCL-4's measures, paired over items (`ccl1.boot`).

| H | Measure | View | NON-INFERIOR when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D3 | Current holders of the offices it was told about | routed | lower bound above −2 points |
| D4 | Offices learned by session 4 and unchanged since | routed | above −2 |
| D5 | Offices first reported by session 12 and unchanged since | routed | above −2 |
| D6 | Dated questions | routed | above −2 |
| D7 | What follows | routed | above −2 |
| D8 | Lies admitted | routed | upper bound below +1 point |
| D9 | GSM8K | weights alone | lower bound above −3 |
| D10 | MMLU | weights alone | above −2 |
| D11 | Never-streamed offices of the world's own half (the other half's are rehearsed by CCL-7's office anchors and neighbour rows; CCL-7's correction of 29 October) | weights alone | above −5 |

**Judging:**
- Item 19 is PASSED when D1, D2 and D3–D11 are all NON-INFERIOR.
- It is FAILED when one interval lies wholly on the wrong side.
- It is NOT SHOWN otherwise.

**Reported:**
- D2 for the weights alone;
- each run's deleted-person measure against the untouched model;
- the cost of the deletion: the sessions trained again, and DELETE's running time against RETRAIN's.

## Validity
A world is valid if:
- both runs finished 34 sessions with the done line;
- both answered every question after session 34;
- DELETE's log shows the removal in session 25, going back to the session its manifest names;
- both have the 20 deleted-person scores for the learner and for the untouched model.

A run stopped by the machine without output is rerun once, in a new folder. Worlds not run by 10 November are left out, and the analysis states how many it pools.
