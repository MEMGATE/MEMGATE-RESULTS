# RV-2 spec: the gate on real editors' real mistakes, with each edit read as the claim it actually makes

**Status:** spec, written 20 October 2026, before the reader or the runner exists.
- The main half's revisions are being fetched now, but none will be inspected before the preregistration is frozen.
- Only their count is used, to report coverage.

## Why
Every confirmatory test so far used simulated sources. RV-1 tried real Wikidata editors and was INVALID (`RV1-RESULT-2026-10-19.md`). It read every edit as "this editor says X holds the office now". Most kept edits fill in history or update a statement after the session date, so honest editors looked like liars.

RV-2 reads each edit as the claim it actually makes.

## Data
- **The edits:** the 9,675 edits of TM-1's offices fetched for RV-1 (`data/wikidata-reverts`, 1 January 2024 to 18 October 2026). Each has a revision id, its parent, a time, a pseudonymous editor and tags.
- **The statements:** `cl/rv2_fetch.py` reads the item at each edit's revision and at its parent, and keeps only the six office properties' statements:
  - the statement id and rank;
  - the value;
  - the start (P580), end (P582) and end-cause (P1534) qualifiers, with each time's precision.
  - 12,791 revisions in all. Hidden or missing revisions are recorded and reported.
- **What the stream does not contain:** qualifier-only API edits, bulk entity edits and reverts. They never name an office property in their summary, so RV-1's fetch never listed them. Their effect is still seen, because each listed edit is read against its own parent revision.

## The claim reader (`cl/rv2_claims.py`)
- **The current holder at a moment `t`, in one revision's statements of one property:**
  - Ignore deprecated statements, and statements whose value is "somevalue" or "novalue".
  - A statement is current at `t` if:
    - its start is missing or no later than `t`; a start of year or month precision counts from the first day of the period;
    - and its end is missing or later than `t`; an end of year or month precision counts to the last day of the period.
  - **Ties:** preferred rank first, then the latest start.
  - The result is one value, none, or ambiguous (several current statements left, with the same rank and start).
- **When an edit is a claim.** An edit by editor `e` at time `t` is the claim "`e` says the current holder is `v`" exactly when:
  - after the edit the current holder at `t` is a single value `v`;
  - and before the edit it was anything else: another value, none, or ambiguous.
  
  Edits that leave the current holder unchanged fill in history or touch other statements, and are not claims. Edits that leave no holder, or an ambiguous one, are counted separately.
- **Truth:** the settled timeline in the 18 October 2026 snapshot, at day precision (TM-1's `segments`). A claim is wrong if `v` is not the holder on the edit's date:
  - a later holder (premature);
  - a past holder;
  - someone never recorded as a holder.
  
  It counts as wrong whether or not Wikidata reverted it.

## The stream, the registry and the arms
- **Offices:** TM-1's 1,341 offices, split by TM-1's hash rule (`se3_world.half`). The dev half is used to build and check the reader, and the main half is run once, after the freeze.
- **Sessions:** monthly, from January 2024 to October 2026 (34 sessions). They are finer than TM-1's quarters, so that the registry is held near each edit's moment. Each claim enters the session containing its date.
- **Sources:** editors, by pseudonym. There are no simulated sources.
- **Registry.** It answers with the holder on the session's last day. Each session:
  - it reports 5% of the offices unasked (TM-1's 15% a quarter, divided by three);
  - it answers the gate's questions within a budget at TM-1's rate: 40 questions per 177 offices per quarter, divided by three per month.
  
  The registry is played by Wikidata's own settled timeline: the consensus of the same editors, in hindsight. RV-2 therefore tests how the gate treats real editors' timing, mistakes and track records, not whether the registry is right.
- **Arms:**
  - **NAIVE** learns every claim.
  - **GATE** is the frozen memgate gate (`memgate/gate.py`) with TM-1's settings.
  - **Descriptive arms:**
    - **GATE-v2** (`cl/gate_v2.py`): the candidate staleness rule;
    - **REGISTRY-ONLY:** it admits only registry values, at the same budget, and spends its questions on the offices that received claims that session, newest first.

## Measures
1. **Wrong claims learned:** the wrong claims an arm admits in the session they arrive. NAIVE learns them all.
2. **Honest editors dismissed:** editors with at least one claim and no wrong claim whom the arm dismisses.
3. **Current accuracy and coverage at each session's end:**
   - accuracy is the share of offices whose admitted value is the holder on that day;
   - coverage is the share of offices with an admitted value.
   
   Both are reported at the last session and averaged over sessions.
4. **Stale values held:** past holders admitted after the last session.
5. **Registry questions asked.**

All measures are counts or shares on one historical stream, so there are no confidence intervals.

## Checks before the freeze (dev half only)
- **Unit tests** of the reader on hand-built statement sets: precision, ranks, ties, deprecated statements, and somevalue / novalue.
- **Hand check of 100 dev edits,** drawn at random with a fixed seed, all of them claims or non-claims as the reader sees them.
  - I write the current holder before and after each edit, and two frontier models do the same blind.
  - The reader must agree with the adjudicated label on at least 95 of 100. Otherwise the reader is fixed, and checked again on a fresh 100.
- **Dev run of all arms,** to set nothing but to catch bugs. Every fix is documented.

## Predictions (to be frozen in RV2-PREREG with the code hash)
1. **GATE learns at most 10% of the wrong claims NAIVE learns** on the main half.
2. **GATE dismisses at most 5 honest editors** on the main half.
3. **GATE's current accuracy at the last session** is at least NAIVE's minus 5 pp, and coverage is reported beside it.

These predictions may be revised after the dev run, before the freeze, with the reason recorded.
