# RS-1 census: what today's models know about who holds 1,341 real offices

**Status:** descriptive measurement, 19 October 2026. This is not a preregistered test. Script: `cl/rs1_probe.py`. Answers: `runs/rs1-census-20261019/`.

## Question

On 19 October 2026, who holds each of TM-1's 1,341 offices? Most of these changed hands between January 2024 and October 2026. The newest frontier models were asked, one office at a time, as a model with no memory and no search would be.

## Protocol

**Offices.** TM-1 world 8000's 1,341 offices (Wikidata snapshot of 18 October 2026):
- 1,278 whose holder changed between January 2024 and October 2026;
- 63 that did not.

**Prompt.**
- Question: "Who currently …?" (TM-1's second phrasing).
- System message: "Answer with the person's full name only. If you do not know, answer: unknown."
- Temperature 0 where the model allows it; the reasoning models used their defaults; claude-opus-5-5 used effort "low".

**Sorting each answer** against Wikidata's record of the office:
- **current:** names the holder on 18 October 2026;
- **past:** names an earlier holder;
- **not in record:** names someone the record never lists;
- **unknown:** answered unknown.

## Results (%)

**The 1,278 offices that changed:**

| Model | Current | Past holder | Not in record | Unknown |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| gpt-6-astra | **43.6** | 19.2 | 8.0 | 29.2 |
| gpt-6.1-sol | **42.6** | 17.9 | 8.7 | 30.7 |
| claude-opus-5-5 | **39.7** | 39.2 | 12.9 | 8.0 |
| grok-4.7 | **13.8** | 47.4 | 3.9 | 34.8 |
| gpt-4.1-nano | **0.5** | 46.9 | 30.3 | 22.1 |

**The 63 offices that did not change:** current 85.7–93.7% for every model.

Seven answers failed after retries: three for Claude, one for each of the other models.

**Still running:** Phi-4-mini-instruct and Ministral-3B, whose deployments allow 1,000 tokens a minute.

## What it shows

- **The best model knows under half.** Even the best model named the current holder for under half of the offices that changed in 2024–2026.
- **Wrong answers are mostly stale.** The models' errors are mostly past holders (lag), not inventions. That is the distinction the gate's "lag is not lying" rule draws. A gate that dismissed every source naming a past holder would dismiss every one of these models.
- **Some answers are not in the record.** These run from 4% to 30% of the changed offices. Some are real holders Wikidata never recorded and some are inventions. They were not audited.

## Limits

- **Record errors.** Wikidata's record is the reference, and it has errors of its own. In FT-2's dev world, a few registry values looked wrong at first sight but are plausible post-2025 appointments.
- **No search.** The models were not allowed to search, and a model with search answers a different question.
- **Phrasing.** One phrasing per office.

- **The frontier models' errors are mostly one change behind:** 59–72% of their wrong answers name the holder just before the current one. Their knowledge stops before the change. Holders of other offices are rare (2–7%).
- **The small models are more confused:** they name older holders and other offices' holders more often.

**An audit of 20 answers outside the record.** 20 offices were drawn with the seed ERROR-KINDS-AUDIT from claude-opus-5-5's and gpt-6.1-sol's "a name the snapshot does not hold" answers, and checked by hand:
- **5 name the recorded holder under another spelling** that the matcher missed:
  - Turchin / Turchyn;
  - Lukashenko / Lukashenka;
  - Musaev / Musayev;
  - De Mezzo Mario-Lucian / Mario De Mezzo;
  - Guillaume Jean Joseph Marie / Guillaume V.
- **2 name a holder newer than Wikidata's record:**
  - Kelly Ayotte, governor of New Hampshire since January 2025 (record: Chris Sununu);
  - Hideaki Nishino at Sony Interactive Entertainment (record: Hiroki Totoki).
- **4 answer another reading of the question:**
  - the state of Salzburg against the city;
  - a different Saint-Pierre;
  - a party's president against its leader, twice.
- **9 are wrong or could not be settled.**

So at least 7 of the 20 were right. **The census's current-holder shares are lower bounds,** and the "not in the record" column mixes real errors with matching and record errors. The comparisons between arms in the preregistered tests use one matcher for every arm, so they are not biased by this. Their absolute rates carry the same small undercount.

**The name recogniser** (`cl/name_match.py`, run by `cl/census_rematch.py`, in [`name-match-audit.json`](../runs/rs1-census-20261019/name-match-audit.json)).
- **What it adds:** it accepts the same person under transliterations, word order, patronymics and hyphenated names, with an edit-distance guard. It was tuned on one sample (seed NAME-MATCH-AUDIT).
- **Hand check:** a fresh sample of 30 new matches (seed NAME-MATCH-CHECK). 27 are the same person. 2 differ in a second surname (Pino Forcada / Pino Lara; Borràs i Ventura / Borràs i Alborch) and are counted as wrong.
- **One "wrong" match was right.** It named Najib Mikati, and Wikidata's record holds him under the English label "Mohamed Agha", with his own name only among the aliases. A record error.
- **How many it moves:**

| Model | Not in record → current | Not in record → past |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| gpt-6-astra | 8 | 6 |
| gpt-6.1-sol | 8 | 4 |
| claude-opus-5-5 | 5 | 8 |
| grok-4.7 | 1 | 6 |
| gpt-4.1-nano | 1 | 8 |
| Ministral-3B | 0 | 1 |

- **Effect on the best model:** gpt-6-astra moves from 557 to 565 of the 1,278 changed offices, 43.6% to 44.2%.
- **Conclusion:** the matcher's undercount is small. The larger, unmeasured part is Wikidata itself lagging behind the world, as in the Ayotte and Nishino answers above.
