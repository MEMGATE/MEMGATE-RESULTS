## What this means

**A last-write-wins memory is easy to poison.** A quarter of the attacked facts were answered with the lie, because colluders and the stealthy poisoner were often the last to speak.

**A frontier reasoning model can protect itself, if it sees everything and is told how.** Given every provenance-tagged report and told that values change and sources lag, it reached 99.8% truth and 1.2% poison at the end.

Three caveats:
1. **It is weak early.** After four sessions, with few honest reports to outvote the attackers, 16.6% of the attacked facts were answered with the lie. The gate was at 0 from the start.
2. **It depends on the instruction.** With a looser instruction on dev ("sources may be mistaken or outdated"), the same model left 27.6% of changed facts at their old value.
3. **It costs a prompt nine times larger,** and that cost grows with the history of each fact.

**The gate gives the result by construction:**
- every report is weighed by its provenance before the model sees anything;
- the model receives one verified value;
- that holds whatever the model and whatever the instruction.

**RM-2 tests what a plain-text memory cannot defend:** forged provenance.

## Limits, stated plainly

- **The world is synthetic.** The companies are invented and the attackers scripted.
- **One model.** A single frontier reasoning model was tested at its default effort. Weaker models and other instructions were not run.
- **H1's margin is small** (−1.25 pp). Over 8 worlds, the CI's upper bound was below 0.
- **The registry is an oracle, as in SE-2.** In RAW, the model was told that "registry" is official, and RM-2 shows what an attacker can do with that.
