# JK-1: jijñāsā (inquiry under doubt) and kṣetrajña (knowing the field of one's competence)

**Status:** design, written on 17 October 2026 before any JK-1 code. No model calls are made.

## 1. Sources and mapping

Ember studied both as mechanism-only engineering bars. Neither was ever compared with a control:

- **Jijñāsā** (`JIJNASA-PHASE2-REPORT-2026-10-12`, SURVIVE). It is the desire to know: inquiry arises where knowledge is lacking or doubtful.
- **Kṣetra–kṣetrajña** (`KSETRAJNA-LITE-REPORT-2026-09-25`, PASS). Bhagavad Gītā 13 distinguishes the field from its knower, and the knower knows the field.

The mappings below are mine.

**JIJNASA, asking only under doubt.** The learner asks for (observes) the true label of a case only when it doubts its own answer. That is:

- the fallback answered, because no lesson decided; or
- the deciding lessons are weakly established, with fewer than 6 supports in total.

When it does not doubt, it neither asks nor learns from that case. Every case is still scored.

**RANDOM-ASK, the control.** The learner asks for exactly as many labels as JIJNASA asked in the same run, at uniformly random cases (`random.Random(seed + 777)`).

**FULL, the reference.** The learner observes every label: ordinary R-ORD.

**KSETRAJNA, knowing where one knows.** Run FULL and answer only the cases a lesson decided; abstain where the fallback would answer. It is measured as selective accuracy, the accuracy on answered cases.

**RANDOM-ABSTAIN, the control.** Answer the same number of cases, chosen uniformly at random (`random.Random(seed + 999)`) from FULL's predictions.

## 3. Hypotheses and decision rules

The decision rules are as before, stratified by dataset.

| Hypothesis | Comparison |
| --- | --- |
| **H1 (primary)** | JIJNASA − RANDOM-ASK accuracy at the same label budget |
| H2 | KSETRAJNA − RANDOM-ABSTAIN selective accuracy at the same coverage |
| Descriptive | FULL − JIJNASA, and the share of labels JIJNASA asked for |

## 4. Predictions before any JK-1 code

| Hypothesis | Prediction | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | positive | Asking where the learner is unsure should beat asking at random. A confident but wrong lesson is never corrected, though, which limits the gain. |
| H2 | strongly positive (WIN) | Lesson-decided cases are much more accurate than fallback cases in every earlier run. |
