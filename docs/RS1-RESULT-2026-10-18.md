## What this means

**The rasa-sūtra, read as a lesson-forming rule, does nothing measurable.**
- The rule required every new lesson to combine at least two documented kinds of evidence: determinants (history and background), consequents (signs at admission), and transitory states (blood values).
- It acted thousands of times. Accuracy did not move, and the documented kinds did no better than random kinds of the same sizes.
- This agrees with O-2: the documented organisation of the inputs is not something this learner can use.

**HCV staging was at chance for every arm** (about 25% over 4 classes). That matches published work on this dataset. The informative test is the myocardial infarction set, where the rule also changed nothing (+0.1 pp).

**The register entry for Nāṭyaśāstra rasa is:**
- tested as a rule for forming lessons;
- no effect;
- the documented kinds of evidence carry nothing beyond what any conjunction carries.

Bharata's point is aesthetic: rasa is a realised state produced by combining elements. It is not an inference about which observations jointly predict an outcome. The mapping, not the source, is what was tested.
