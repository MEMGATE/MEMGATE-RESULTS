**Plain reading.** When a language model writes lessons from its own experience and sees the matching lessons later, it predicts new cases 3.6–3.8 pp better than with retrieved past cases alone. This holds for both models and in all three families. It is the first preregistered real-model continual-learning WIN in this project.

Managing those lessons with the Yoga vṛtti filter, or adding apavāda and satpratipakṣa, did not improve accuracy further.

## Why the filter did not help, although filtered lessons are better

| Arm | Predictions that showed lessons | Their accuracy |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| ORD | 3,120 | 81.0% |
| VRTTI | 2,314 | 84.5% |
| SHASTRA | 2,150 | 87.4% |

The vṛtti filter rejected 890 of Astra's proposals (71.5% of non-duplicates) and 875 of Sol's (70.3%) as **viparyaya**: experience had already contradicted them.

The lessons that remained are more reliable but appear less often. On the other cases the model falls back on the retrieved cases. The two effects cancel.

Under label noise the filter costs about 1.6 pp. Noise makes some useful general rules look contradicted, so the filter rejects them. This is the same pattern as in SR-1 and SR-2: keeping general rules is what tolerates noise.

## Claude Opus 5.5 (from the dev run)

Opus 5.5 refused 75 of 961 dev calls (7.8%). Every refusal was a prediction call and returned `stop_reason: "refusal"` with category `cyber`, although the prompts contained only anonymised `f0=v1 … -> L1` cases.

The refusals fell unevenly across arms, so Opus was replaced by gpt-6.1-sol before the main run (see the preregistration). This matters for Pramana: Opus 5.5's safety classifier can block benign symbolic-learning prompts.

## What this means for Pramana

1. **Model-written lessons work in this setting.** An experience → lesson → application cycle with real models measurably beats storage plus retrieval on unseen cases, in anonymised symbolic worlds where prior knowledge cannot help.
2. **No śāstra management benefit here.** No benefit was measured for Yoga vṛtti admission, apavāda or satpratipakṣa on top of that cycle. Validation makes lessons more precise but less available.
3. **Most model-written rules are already wrong.** About 70% of them contradict stored experience. A system that stores them unvalidated keeps a mostly-false memory, and the model still copes by weighting it against the retrieved cases. Whether that remains harmless at larger scale is untested.

## Limits

- Synthetic anonymised worlds, two OpenAI-family models, 100 cases per world, six worlds per stratum.
- One prompt design.
- No natural-language tasks and no Pramana M-CORE integration.
