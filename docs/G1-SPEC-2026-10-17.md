# G-1: Śrī Cakra and Vāstu ring capacities as allocation of a bounded lesson memory

**Status:** design, written on 17 October 2026 before any G-1 code was run. G-1 makes no model calls.

## 1. What is tested, and why this form

**The question.** A continual learner with a fixed memory budget must decide which lessons to keep. Two sacred diagrams prescribe how a bounded space is divided among concentric enclosures:

- **The Śrī Cakra's triangle rings.** The traditional counts give 43 triangles: the central triangle, then rings of 8, 10, 10 and 14.
- **The Vāstu Puruṣa 81-pada grid.** From the centre outward: Brahmā's 9 central padas, then rings of 16, 24 and 32 padas (Mānasāra VII; see Codex's Vāstu reading).

Does dividing a bounded lesson memory by specificity level in these proportions keep better lessons than ordinary retention?

**Why not the exact geometry.** The exact interlocking geometry of the nine triangles (which small regions lie inside which triangles) is not tested. Codex's source reading records that no coordinates or region enumeration have been derived from a primary text, and an invented construction would test my invention, not the yantra. That geometry stays **BLOCKED** in the coverage register until YH III.95–97 and Amṛtānanda's commentary are collated. The ring counts are what the sources do support.

## 2. Levels and the two readings

A lesson's specificity level is the number of its conditions:

| Level | Conditions | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | 1 | most general |
| L2 | 2 | |
| L3 | 3 | |
| L4+ | 4 or more | most specific |

**Reading C (centre = general).** This follows *sṛṣṭi-krama*, emanation from the bindu: unity at the centre, multiplicity at the periphery. The few general lessons take the inner rings, and the many specific lessons take the outer rings.

**Reading O (outer = general).** This is the reading of the earlier Ember SC design ("inner = most relevant"): general lessons take the outer rings.

## 3. Arms

Every bounded arm keeps at most **B = 43 validated lessons**, the Śrī Cakra's triangle count. When a pool or a level is full, the lesson with the lowest support is evicted, ties going to the oldest. An evicted lesson may re-form later.

| Arm | Quotas L1 / L2 / L3 / L4+ |
| --- | --- |
| R-ORD | unbounded (reference) |
| FLAT | one pool of 43, lowest support evicted (ordinary retention) |
| UNIFORM | 11 / 11 / 11 / 10 |
| SRI-C | 9 / 10 / 10 / 14 (central 8 + 1 for the most general) |
| SRI-O | 14 / 10 / 10 / 9 |
| VASTU-C | 5 / 8 / 13 / 17 (9 : 16 : 24 : 32 scaled to 43 by largest remainder) |
| VASTU-O | 17 / 13 / 8 / 5 |
| EXT-DT, EXT-NB, EXT-1NN | unbounded context |

Every bounded arm uses R-ORD's formation, revision and conflict rules. Only the retention differs.

## 5. Hypotheses and decision rules

The decision rules are the same as in SR-1 to SR-3.

| Hypothesis | Comparison |
| --- | --- |
| **H1 (primary)** | SRI-C − FLAT on the six held-out datasets |
| H2 | VASTU-C − FLAT |
| H3 | SRI-C − UNIFORM and VASTU-C − UNIFORM: does the geometry add anything beyond levelling? |
| H4 | SRI-O − FLAT and VASTU-O − FLAT (the other reading) |
| Descriptive | FLAT − R-ORD, the cost of the budget |

**INVALID if** the budget never evicts anything in the sacred arms on the held-out data, because then the mechanism was not exercised.

## 6. Predictions before any G-1 code

- **Where the budget binds.** Mostly on KRK (18 classes) and on flare and balance. Elsewhere, the arms will tie with R-ORD.
- **H1 and H2: NO WIN.** Fixed level quotas force weak lessons to be kept in sparse levels while strong lessons are evicted from crowded ones. Support-based FLAT should be better or equal.
- **H3: ≈ 0.**
- **H4: NO WIN.**
