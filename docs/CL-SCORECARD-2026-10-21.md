# Continual learning, the whole ideal: where the lab stands

**Written:** 21 October 2026, 18:40 IST; updated 22:20, and on 22 October at 04:25, 07:09, 08:27, 12:58, 13:15, 15:55 and 23:35, and on 23 October at 03:35, 04:15, 06:10, 11:00, 11:15, 17:08 and 17:35, and on 24 October at 11:06. Internal.

This is a map from each problem and property in the field summary the founder shared, to the lab's evidence. Every entry points to a result document. A preregistered result counts as evidence; an exploration or a plan does not. A descriptive check, a measurement with no hypothesis, is marked as one. "Open" means not yet tested, or tested without a result.

## The known problems

| Problem | Status | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Catastrophic forgetting | **answered for facts; measured at frontier scale** | TM-1: naive continual fine-tuning cut offices never in the stream from 99.2% to 9.2%; through the layer they stayed at 98.3%. This holds by design, since the layer controls where each update applies. RP-3 (22 Oct): one hosted fine-tuning job took GPT-4.1's untouched offices from 90.3% to 25.3–29.7%, and gpt-4.1-nano's from 89.3% to 0.0%. FT-2 and CD-1 (continual GPT-4.1 with and without the layer) are still running. |
| 2. Stability against plasticity | **answered, for facts** | TM-1: the layer learned changes about as well as naive fine-tuning (92.4% against 93.4%, within the margin) and kept untouched knowledge. |
| 3. Interference and retrieval collision | **answered** | SC-2: a memory that keeps old versions without dates made six models name a past holder 25–84% of the time; the layer's current-value memory gave 99.5–100% current. GI-1: lessons placed in every prompt did no harm to unrelated answers (+5.44 pp). **SC-3 (22 Oct), at scale:** retrieval over every office (11,611 lines) cost 1.67 pp and adding every past holder 2.92 pp more (stale 1.0–5.7%); the layer's keyed current value recovered +3.67 pp (WIN), within 1.3 pp of each question's own line. An early check (SC-1: no collision in 60 of 60) had too easy a design and no preregistration, so it is not counted. |
| 4. Scalability and cost | **answered for memory; measured for weights** | EF-1 (descriptive): an update to 300,000 facts in memory takes 40 ms, with decisions identical to the original gate. Hosted fine-tuning took 1.7–5.2 h per block. |
| 5. Evaluation gap (real, stateful streams) | **answered on real data and on a public benchmark; a live run is under way** | RV-2: real Wikidata editors, 2 of 149 wrong values learned. Real office changes 2024–26 and real Python library changes. LS-1 is capturing the live stream for 7 days. **LM-1 (23 Oct), a public benchmark:** on LongMemEval (402 held-out questions, blind hand labels), the layer's dated memory added to retrieval raised accuracy from 72.8% to 83.9% (GPT-4.1) and from 77.1% to 94.9% (gpt-6.1-sol), H1 WIN on both; against the whole history in context, +9.35 and +0.28 with a third of the tokens (H3 NON-INFERIOR on both); time questions against the whole history not shown (H2). **LM-1 replicates (23 Oct, descriptive):** claude-opus-5-5 +15.86 and grok-4.7 +14.16 points; three small open models +5.67, +4.25 and +0.28. |
| 6. Discovery against reuse | **answered** | DR-2: agents that discovered working migrations themselves (kept only if they passed the checks) then solved 32.5 pp more unseen programs, in 2.4 fewer actions, close to a complete verified library. |
| 7. LLM-specific: context limits, shift, durable against temporary | **answered; durable against temporary, in a trained model too** | The layer's memory prompt was 15–19 times smaller than escaping or spotlighting (42 tokens a question). Real non-stationary changes throughout. Dated, superseding memory (RP-1, SC-2). **CN-1 (22 Oct):** choosing what to train into the weights by how likely it is to keep holding cut reversals from 7.05% (newest first, as fine-tuning on updates does) to 1.11% of values trained, with no loss of coverage (model-free, 4,746 offices). **CN-2 (22 Oct):** the same rule in a trained 7B model's answers cut stale answers from 0.61% to 0.12% of offices (H1 WIN, 20 of 20 worlds) and named more current holders (+4.62 pp), when the set in the weights is rehearsed; replicated on OLMo-2-7B (0.54% to 0.11%, 10 of 10 worlds). **TQ-1 (22 Oct):** a dated memory line answered questions about a date on both sides of a change (86–100%); a memory with only the current value led two models to name the current holder for 94–97% of past dates (H1 +50.94 pp WIN). **TW-1 (23 Oct), the weights route on time, three open 7–8B families:** a change fine-tuned in with its date still overwrote the past (95–100% of past-date questions named the new holder); the dated memory line answered both dates for 17–68% of offices against at most 0.3% (H1 WIN on all three). Practising date questions on 150 offices taught the weights to use a trained date on 150 others (38–45% right against 0–0.7%), at a cost to present answers. |

## The ideal properties

| Property | Status | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hybrid:** memory for fast, reversible change; selective, safe weight updates for durable knowledge | **answered: memory, weights, and which goes where** | TM-1 (weights, through the layer); UNDO-1 (exact removal from weights: the model scores removed lies as a never-poisoned model does). RP-2: weights alone do not carry a fact to what follows from it; memory does (RP-1). MC-1, the whole layer end to end with no registry: 0.0% attacker values in four models' answers (against 9.4–11.7% with a memory that trusts its input), better follow-on answers, and 17.9 pp fewer current holders named (the cost of needing agreement). **PL-1 (23 Oct):** a line for what two or more independent established sources report but memory has not verified yet won back 5.21 pp of current holders (H1 WIN, four models, a new world) with no attacker's value named (H2 SAFE); a 9.9 pp gap to the naive memory remains. **22 Oct:** RP-3, weights carry a change when trained with what follows from it; CH-1, memory composes and weights do not (+36.05 pp); CN-1, what to move into the weights (reversals 7.05% to 1.11%); CN-2, the same in a trained model's answers (stale 0.61% to 0.12%). **23 Oct, time:** TW-1, dated facts trained into the weights still overwrite the past; TW-2, the skill of reading the dated memory line, trained into three open 7–8B models on other offices, answered both dates right for 98.3–100% of new offices (17.3–68.3% untouched), with no loss of untouched knowledge: facts in memory, the skill in the weights. TW-3: trained only on football coaches, the skill carried to other kinds of office and to a wording never seen (both dates 89.8–100%; H1 WIN on all three), with a 3–4 point cost in untouched knowledge on two models. TW-4: with four neighbouring lines, a reader trained on a mix of memory sizes answered 83.7–95.7% (H1 WIN on all three); a one-line reader fell to 53.7–74.3%. **GC-1 (24 Oct), the skill's cost to general knowledge:** on MMLU (1,140 questions), the trained reader stayed non-inferior at −2 points for Qwen2.5-7B (−0.79) and OLMo-2-7B (+2.46), and cost Nemotron-Nano 2.81 points (H1 not shown). |

## What is open, and what each needs
- **Transfer in weights: answered (RP-3 main, 22 Oct).** GPT-4.1 fine-tuned also on what follows from a change carried new facts trained without those statements better: +20.22 pp [15.78, 24.67], WIN. Replicated on mini (+15.78), nano (+5.33) and Llama-3.3-70B (+5.11), and with the lab's own LoRA loop on two open families, Qwen2.5-7B (+5.11, WIN) and OLMo-2-7B (+4.89, WIN) ([RP3-RESULT-OPEN-LORA-2026-10-22.md](RP3-RESULT-OPEN-LORA-2026-10-22.md)).
  - Every fine-tuning job cost untouched knowledge: GPT-4.1 went from 90.3% to 25.3–29.7%, and nano from 89.3% to 0.0%.
- **Scale beyond 7B for open weights:** done through Azure's hosted training, which needs no GPU quota.
  - Llama-3.3-70B was used in RP-3 (H1–H4 WIN) and CH-1.
  - gpt-oss-120b trained but cannot be served without provisioned capacity.
  - The lab's own training loop now runs on rented GPUs at Modal (22 Oct; Azure's GPU quota is still 0). RP-3 on Qwen2.5-7B is done. The 72B runs are on hold by the founder's decision of 23 October, with no Modal spending for now (`docs/GPU-PLAN-2026-10-21.md`). Since 22 October the loop also runs on Kaggle's two T4s, for the 7–8B tests TW-1 to TW-5. Continual fine-tuning of a fine-tuned open model is not offered by the hosted service; on Modal the lab's loop does it.
- **A live stream:** LS-1's capture ends on 28 October. Its outcomes are read about 30 days later.

## How to read this
- **The facts are office holders and Python library usages; the skills are code migrations and one rule-based tax procedure** (PR-1, 22 Oct: a changed procedure applied from memory, 0% to 100% for a model that did not know it; a forged update in a memory that joins whatever it receives took over five models' answers, +81.4 pp WIN).
- **Chains through a change: answered for two steps (CH-1, 22 Oct) and three steps in memory (CH-2, 22 Oct: +52.0 pp WIN; 95–99% of the chains a model could complete).** The layer's memory composed with what six models know: +43.76 pp, WIN, and 86–98% of known items. The same facts trained into the weights did not compose in short answers: memory beat the fine-tuned models by +36.05 pp, WIN. They reached 42–47% only when asked to name the person first.
- **The attackers are the lab's own designs.** No outside red team has seen the rules.
