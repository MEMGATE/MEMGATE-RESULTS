## What this means

**The model knows the field of its competence** (Gītā 13).
- Asked only to add SURE or UNSURE to its answer, the model drew a useful line. Its SURE answers were right 72–88% of the time, and its UNSURE answers about half the time.
- Answering only when SURE gave answers +8.33 pp more accurate than answering as many random cases.
- This matters for ML-6's gap. There a model inside an assembly had no doubt signal of its own, and the assembly needed a warm-up and disagreement among its members to ask. ML-7 shows the model can supply that signal itself.

**Asking when unsure beats asking at random, at a price.**
- At the same number of labels (21–29%), it was +2.92 pp better than random asking.
- It stayed 4.94 pp below the model that saw every label.
- The model says SURE on about two thirds of cases and is not always right when it does. That includes DRIFT after the switch, the blind spot MX-3 fixed with audits.

**For a working AI system built on a language model:**
- ask the model to say whether it is sure;
- answer alone only when it is;
- ask a person when it is not;
- as MX-3 and MX-4 showed, still spot-check a share of its sure answers, because a model can be sure and wrong after the world changes.
