# MX-12: an alarm that also recovers. Pause on vikṛti, resume when prakṛti returns

**Status:** design, written on 18 October 2026 before any MX-12 code. No model calls are made.

## 1. Question

MX-11's alarm made the two-failure case safe (0.84 wrong answers alone per 100). Once fired, though, it stopped answering for good. Its rare false alarms, and its alarms after a single failure that dismissal would have handled, therefore cost credence (HARM by the strict rule).

**Caraka's reading.** Caraka's prakṛti–vikṛti is a diagnosis, not a sentence: the treatment ends when the patient returns to their constitution. MX-12 adds that return.

**The mechanism.**
- **On the alarm,** answering alone pauses. Three-way splits keep being recorded.
- **Resume.** Once at least 30 cases have passed, if the split rate over the last 30 is back within 1 SE of the pre-alarm baseline, answering resumes. The baseline is the rate `unwell` compared against when it fired, with SE = sqrt(q(1−q)/30) and q = max(baseline, 0.05).
- **After a resume,** the split record starts afresh, so the alarm can fire again later.

## 3. Data and conditions

- **Worlds.** VIEWS at the new seeds 54000–54019, 400 cases each.
- **Conditions.** SOUND, ONE and TWO, failing from case 240.
- **Testability condition.** As before.

## 4. Hypotheses and decision rules

| Hypothesis | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **H1 (safety)** | RECOVER in TWO is SAFE if the upper bound of the 95% CI of its wrong answers alone after case 240 is below 2 per 100. |
| **H2 (cost, SOUND)** | RECOVER − SUPERVISOR, net credence. NO HARM if the CI lower bound is above −1.0 pp. |
| H3 (cost, ONE) | the same, in ONE |
| Descriptive | RECOVER − ALARM in each condition; alarms, resumes and the time spent paused |

**INVALID if:**
- any run is non-deterministic or crashes;
- the copy, with recovery off, differs from MX-11's function;
- the testability condition fails.

## 5. Predictions

| Hypothesis | Prediction | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | SAFE | With two broken sources the split rate stays high, so the pause holds. |
| H2 | NO HARM | False alarms resume within about 30 cases. |
| H3 | NO HARM | After a single failure, dismissal removes the broken source, splits fall, and answering resumes. |
