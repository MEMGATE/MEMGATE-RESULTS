## What this means

**The mixed system works, on a real machine.**
- On the hydraulic rig, three groups of sensors watched one machine: pressure, flow and vibration, temperature. One group failed mid-stream.
- The supervisor answered alone 18–78% of cycles, depending on the target, at 96.7–99.8% accuracy. The plain network managed 8–51%.
- The supervisor:
  - acts when the three groups agree and none is guessing;
  - dismisses the group that is the odd one out;
  - keeps refuted lessons latent until they are confirmed again.

**Two pieces earn their place inside the whole.**
- **Dismissal (Arthaśāstra 1.12):** +11.82.
- **Saṃskāra (Yoga Sūtra 4.8–4.9):** +2.56. On real sensor data a lesson broken by one noisy reading comes back after three confirmations, so the spies guess less.

**Two pieces do not pay at the preregistered price.** Here a wrong answer given alone costs twice a question (net credence).
- **Audits:** −0.96. The guessing clause already keeps wrong answers given alone below 1% on the rig, so audits mostly take answers away.
- **Resets:** −1.00. They throw away learned lessons. They do cut the wrong answers given alone (−0.56 pp), most where rules switch (VIEWS-RECUR: 7.3% without resets vs 4.5% with).

**Where audits and resets would pay.** Suppose a right answer alone is worth 1, a question 0, and a wrong answer alone costs c.
- Resets pay if c is above about 2.8.
- Audits pay only if c is above about 10.

So a site where a confident mistake is very costly should keep resets, and perhaps audits. A site where questions are cheap need not.

**For a working system**, the tested recipe is:
- several independent cameras or sensors;
- act only on agreement without guessing;
- ask the owner otherwise;
- dismiss the source that keeps disagreeing;
- let a rule broken by one odd reading return after three confirmations;
- add resets where a confident mistake costs more than about three right answers.
