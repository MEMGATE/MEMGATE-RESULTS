## What this means

**The first sentence holds.**
- When three spies watching different sources (three feature sets of a digit; chest, ankle and arm sensors) report the same label and none is guessing, the report is right 96–99.7% of the time.
- That is 16.61 pp more often than the network's answers on as many random cases.

**The second sentence holds, and it matters most when a source fails.**
- **Without dismissal.** After a source broke at the middle case, three agreeing reports became rare. On the real data the network answered alone only 6–7% of cases instead of 16–19%, and asked a person about everything else.
- **With dismissal.** The rule "dismiss the spy that is the odd one in 20 of the last 30 splits" found the broken source in 36 of 40 failing runs, about 30 cases after the failure on real data. With the broken spy dismissed, the network answered alone 19–23% of cases at 98–99% accuracy.
- **The choice of spy matters.** Dismissing a random spy at the same moment recovered much less (+9.78 pp in favour of the right choice).

**Not predicted: dismissal also helped when every source worked** (+1.14).
- On MHEALTH, a weaker sensor was dismissed in 6 of 10 sound runs. The two remaining sensors agreed more often at the same accuracy.
- The cost is a slightly lower accuracy alone after a dismissal: 98.1% vs 99.2% on failing Multiple Features.

**For a working system** (several cameras or sensors watching one site):
- act alone only when independent sources agree and none is guessing;
- ask a person otherwise;
- keep a count of which source is the odd one out. A source that is the odd one out in two thirds of recent disagreements is broken or badly placed. Stop trusting it and tell the owner.

In these tests, that found a broken source within about 30 cases on real sensor data, and kept credence nearly at its sound level.
