## What this means

**Ṣaṭcakra absorption and return.** Absorbing specific lessons into a general one, and restoring them when it fails, is computationally real: hundreds of absorptions and restorations per stream. But it is neutral for accuracy. A general lesson that is clean on memory covers almost the same cases as the specific lessons it replaced. The *order* of absorption, level by level as in the laya-krama or at random, made no difference at all.

**Viṣṇu Purāṇa dissolution.** Destroying the weakest third and vacating the middle third every 200 cases costs 0.33 pp. On the real streams it costs less (−0.17) but still does not help. These 1,000-case windows do not drift fast enough to repay forgetting.

**Kiraṇa niyati.** Giving an outcome only to the lessons that decided a case costs 0.20 pp. Lessons that lose a conflict are never corrected, so the conflict recurs.

**Kālacakra on real drift.** The recency fallback helped on Elec2 (+0.92 pp), the classic drift benchmark. It hurt on gas drift (−1.38), where each window is stationary after the within-batch shuffle. That leaves −0.20 pp on the streams overall. The no-change baseline is close to R-ORD only on Elec2 (85.1 vs 86.1), which confirms the strong label autocorrelation that Žliobaitė (2011) reported there.
