## What this means

**The pariṣad result is now replicated on two independent held-out pools.**

| Experiment | Gain over the lesson learner |
| --- | --- |
| P-1 | +1.44 pp |
| P-2, H3 | +1.56 pp |
| P-2, with the mechanisms inside (H1) | +1.60 pp |

Each passes the full decision rule. A plurality of three learners of different kinds (rules, probabilities, trees) is a reliable improvement over any one of them on these real datasets.

**The small śāstra gains inside the lesson learner do not survive the assembly.**

- On its own, R-ORD with anupalabdhi and akhyāti is +0.29 pp better, significantly so.
- Inside the assembly it adds only +0.04 pp. The other two members already correct most of the errors those mechanisms fix.

**More members of mixed kinds is not better.** Adding 1-NN and the Nyāya–Mīmāṃsā rule policy (PARISHAD-5) cost 0.50 pp. Manu's point is about *different* competences, not about count, and these two members largely repeat the instance-based and rule-based views the assembly already has.

## Limits

- Shuffled data and the H-1 binning rule.
- On the exposed streams in dev, the assembly helped less (+0.04 to +0.61 pp).
- All learners are simple and model-free. The comparison is with the lab's own lesson learner, not with modern ensembles such as boosting.
