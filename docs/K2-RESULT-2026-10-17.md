## Symmetry check over the whole file

The jungle file holds 44,819 positions.

- **714 positions are their own mirror:** both pieces stand on the centre file.
- **44,104 positions have their mirror image in the file, and every one of the pairs has the same outcome.**
- **One position's mirror is absent:** white and black elephants on files 1 and 0 of rank 0.

The mapping (the board is symmetric left to right) is therefore exact for this dataset.

## What this means

**The kolam reading is confirmed on fresh data.** The outcome does not depend on the board's orientation, so each observed case can also be taken in as its symmetric image. This is the reading of K-1, which was exploratory and used exposed boards. It now holds in a preregistered test.

**The lesson learner gained more than 1-NN, against my prediction.** In tic-tac-toe, 1-NN gained most (+17.5 pp). The likely reason is how often a later case is exactly the image of an earlier one:

- In tic-tac-toe, eight symmetries over 958 positions make that frequent, and 1-NN simply recalls the image.
- Here, 1,000 cases are drawn from 44,819 positions, so a case's mirror was among the earlier cases at most about 2% of the time. The gain must come from generalisation, where lessons carry further than a nearest neighbour.

## Limits

- **One dataset and one symmetry.**
- **The best case.** The labels are exact (an exhaustive endgame table), which favours symmetry. Real data rarely has an exact symmetry.
- **Not modelled.** The kolam drawing grammar itself, as distinct from its grid symmetry.
