# LS-1 spec: learning from a live public edit stream, with real vandalism

**Status:** spec, 21 October 2026, 17:55 IST, written when the capture started and before any outcome was fetched. Revised at 18:25, still before any outcome: the primary outcome now follows RV-2 (see below).
**Code:** `cl/ls1_capture.py`, running detached for 168 hours. Its output is `runs/ls1-capture-20261021/` and its log `/tmp/ls1-capture.log`.

## Why
**What exists.** RV-2 tested the gate on real Wikidata editors' past edits from 2024–26, replayed month by month. The gate learned 2 of the 149 wrong values that believing every edit would have learned (`docs/RV2-RESULT-2026-10-20.md`).

**What LS-1 adds.** It runs on the live stream as the edits arrive, on every item with an office property, not only the snapshot's offices. Its record is made at the time, including what was known about each editor then.

## The stream
Wikimedia's public recent-changes stream gives every Wikidata edit as it happens. LS-1 keeps the edits whose summary names one of the lab's office properties:
- head of government (P6);
- head coach (P286);
- head of state (P35);
- chairperson (P488);
- chief executive officer (P169);
- director or manager (P1037).

For each edit it records:
- the item, the revision and the editor;
- whether the editor is anonymous or a bot;
- the edit summary, which names the property and the value;
- the time the edit arrived.

Once a minute it also records what the API says about each new registered editor: edit count, registration date and groups. A rule tested later can therefore use only what was known when the edit arrived.

## The outcome, fetched only later
- **Primary: was the edit's claim right?** RV-2 found that Wikidata reverted only 13 of its wrong claims, so a revert alone misses most mistakes.
  - Each edit is read against its parent revision with RV-2's claim reader, which agreed with two blind labellers on 100 of 100.
  - Its claim is then checked against the item's dated statements as they stand at least 30 days later: who held the office on the edit's date.
- **Secondary: was the edit reverted?** Wikidata marks such an edit with the "mw-reverted" tag.
- **When it is fetched:** at least 30 days after the last captured edit. For the main part, only after the preregistration is frozen.

## Plan
**Dev** is the first 48 hours of edits. Their outcomes are fetched about 30 days after capture ends, and the rules' settings are chosen on them.

**Main** is the remaining 120 hours. The preregistration is frozen before their outcomes are fetched. It fixes the rules, their settings, the measures and the decision rules.

**Rules to compare** (settings fixed on dev):
- **NAIVE:** take every edit at once.
- **ESTABLISHED:** take an edit at once when its editor had a registered account with a record of edits, at least N edits over at least D days, when the edit arrived.
- **PERSIST-H:** take an edit once its value has stood H hours without being reverted.
- **COMBINED:** established editors at once; everyone else after H hours.

**Measures:**
- **Wrong values admitted:** the share of the edits a rule took whose claim was wrong (and, secondarily, that were later reverted).
- **Right values kept:** the share of the right claims that a rule took within 24 hours.
- **Delay:** the median delay before a rule takes an edit.

## Limits known now
- **The primary outcome reads claims against later dated statements.** A claim that no later statement settles is reported apart. Reverts miss most mistakes (RV-2), and some good edits are reverted in disputes.
- **Volume is uncertain.** The first 90 seconds of capture held no office edits. If a week holds too few reverted edits to test anything, LS-1 reports what it saw, descriptively.
- **Values are taken from edit summaries.** Editor details come from the API within about a minute of the edit.
