## Dev pilot (exposed seeds 0–4 only)

The pilot used 5 families × 5 seeds × 9 arms, 225 runs in total, and took 7 s. The full output is in [`runs/dev-20261017-a/SUMMARY.md`](../runs/dev-20261017-a/SUMMARY.md).

R-SHASTRA − R-ORD, in percentage points (95% CI over 5 seeds):

| Family | Difference | 95% CI |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| EXC | −0.45 | [−2.36, +1.45] |
| UPA | +0.15 | [−0.10, +0.45] |
| DNF | −0.60 | [−2.21, +0.95] |
| NOISE | +1.25 | [−0.10, +2.61] |
| DRIFT | −3.41 | [−5.36, −1.50] |

Two of the predictions recorded in the spec already point the wrong way on the dev seeds: EXC was expected to be > 0 and NOISE < 0. Both predictions stay on record unchanged.

Ablations on dev:

- Removing apavāda precedence (noM) costs 6–9 pp in every family.
- Saṃśaya (noS) and satpratipakṣa (noN3) change accuracy by at most 0.2 pp.

Diagnostics from `scripts/diagnose_dev.py`, dev seeds only:

- **General lessons are kept with carved-out exceptions.** This helps under label noise, because there is less fragmentation and less fallback. It hurts when a general lesson is genuinely too broad (DNF) or outdated (DRIFT). R-SHASTRA's formation lessons answer 76.9% right in DRIFT, against 89.3% for R-ORD.
- **Single-candidate upādhi narrowing misfires in EXC:** 78% right over 50 decisions. EXC has no hidden condition, and three to five supports can share a literal by chance.
- **Apavāda candidates with one support are close to coin flips** under noise and in DNF (39–52% right).

No implementation bug was found. Each of these behaviours follows from the spec as written.

## Decision after the pilot

The mechanisms, parameters and arms stay unchanged after the dev pilot, and the main run tests SR-1 exactly as specified.

The pilot suggested three improvements, all deferred to a separate SR-2 with new held-out data:

- A balīyastva guard that retires a general lesson once its exceptions outnumber its confirmations.
- More evidence before a single upādhi is accepted.
- Letting unvalidated exceptions bar the general lesson without deciding the case themselves.

## Main run

Run once, from `claude_lab/`:

```
python -m shastra.run_sr1 main --prereg docs/SR1-PREREG-2026-10-17.md --out runs/main-20261017
```

Instances and checks:

- Synthetic: 5 families × seeds 1000–1019, 100 instances.
- Real: 4 datasets × orders 2000–2009, 40 instances.
- Every arm runs on every instance.
- For a determinism check, the first instance of each group is re-run for every arm.

If the run crashes, the cause is fixed and recorded here and in `LOG.md`, and the run is repeated into a new folder. A completed run is never re-run or overwritten.

## Decision rules (unchanged from spec §5)

**H1 (primary): R-SHASTRA vs R-ORD on the four real datasets.**

Δ is the mean over datasets of the mean paired accuracy difference over the 10 orders. The 95% CI comes from a stratified bootstrap over orders (10,000 resamples, seed 12345).

| Verdict | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| WIN | Δ ≥ +1.0 pp and CI lower bound > 0 |
| NO WIN | CI upper bound < +1.0 pp |
| UNDETERMINED | anything else |
| INVALID | a failed run, non-determinism, or zero N2/M executions on real data |

**H2: R-SHASTRA vs EXT-1NN on real data.** Same rules as H1.

**H3 (descriptive): R-SHASTRA vs EXT-DT and EXT-NB.**

**Synthetic results are diagnostic.** They are reported per family with bootstrap CIs over the 20 seeds.
