# MX-3: the whole system in a changing world: asking under doubt, answering when of one mind, auditing, and the prakṛti–vikṛti check

**Status:** design, written on 18 October 2026 before any MX-3 code. No model calls are made.

## 1. Question

MX-2's system has three parts:
- an assembly of three different learners;
- it asks a person only under doubt (members disagree, or the lesson member doubts);
- it answers on its own only when the three are of one mind.

The system has a blind spot.
- **When the world changes abruptly,** its members may stay of one mind on the old concept. Then they never doubt, never ask, and keep answering wrongly.
- **The prakṛti–vikṛti check** (PK-1, PK-2) detects an abrupt change from the learner's own recent errors. But errors are only seen when a label is seen.

**The proposal.**
- **Audit:** a small share of the unanimous cases is also checked with a person.
- **Watch:** the check runs on the errors of every case whose label the system sees.
- **Reset:** on vikṛti, all members are rebuilt from the latest observed cases.

This is the Āyurvedic reading of PK-1 applied to the whole assembly. Here a person is the source of the labels: the system consults the person when it doubts, and spot-checks itself even when it does not.

## 2. Arms

Every arm is PARISHAD-3* (R-ORD+ANU+AKH, naive Bayes, the decision tree), answering by plurality and asking under doubt.

| Arm | Audits | Health check and reset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GRAND | none | none (MX-2's system) |
| GRAND+AUDIT | 5% of the unanimous cases, drawn by `random.Random(seed + 4242)` | yes |
| GRAND+AUDIT-NORESET | the same audits | none (a control for the audit labels alone) |

**The health check** is PK-1's rule, unchanged, applied to the assembly's errors on the observed cases:
- W = 30, three standard errors, floor 0.05;
- at least 2W observed cases since the last reset.

**The reset** rebuilds all three members from the latest 30 observed cases.

**Scoring.**
- An *autonomous* answer is a case the system neither asked about nor audited: its members are of one mind, and the lesson member does not doubt. Its accuracy is the main measure.
  - *Clarified before the dev run.* The first wording said "a unanimous case that was not audited". But a unanimous case is still asked about when the lesson member doubts, so it is not answered alone. The code always used this definition.
  - MX-2's GRAND measure also counted unanimous cases that were asked about, so MX-3's GRAND is a stricter subset of it.
- *Coverage* is the share of scored cases answered autonomously.
- *Labels* is the share of cases asked about or audited.

## 3. Data

**Main.** Synthetic worlds at new seeds 37000–37019 (400 cases each):
- DRIFT, whose rules switch at case 200;
- the stable families EXC, DNF, UPA and NOISE.

**Scope.** No fresh real stream is used. Real streams with natural labels are scarce, and all those found so far are exposed. MX-3 is a test of the system's behaviour under a known abrupt switch, and of its cost where nothing changes.

**Dev.** The synthetic dev seeds and the nine exposed real streams (SR-5, PK-1, PK-2).

## 4. Hypotheses and decision rules

The decision rules are as before. On DRIFT, the CI is over its 20 seeds. On the stable worlds, it is stratified by family.

| Hypothesis | Comparison (autonomous accuracy) |
| --- | --- |
| **H1 (primary)** | GRAND+AUDIT − GRAND on DRIFT |
| H2 | GRAND+AUDIT − GRAND+AUDIT-NORESET on DRIFT (does the reset matter beyond the audit labels?) |
| H3 | GRAND+AUDIT − GRAND on the stable families (the cost where nothing changes) |
| Descriptive | coverage, labels, resets; accuracy over all cases |

## 5. Predictions before any MX-3 code

| Hypothesis | Prediction |
| --- | --- |
| H1 | positive, WIN. After the switch, GRAND's unanimous answers are wrong until disagreement brings labels. |
| H2 | positive |
| H3 | ≈ 0, with about 5% more labels |
