# AR-2: employment by ascertained purity, giving each case to the member proved on that kind of work (Arthaśāstra 1.10)

**Status:** design, written on 18 October 2026 before any AR-2 code. No model calls are made.

## 1. Source and mapping

**Arthaśāstra 1.10** (Shamasastry's translation, 1915, Book I, Chapter X, "Ascertaining by temptations the purity or impurity in the character of ministers"; checked on Wikisource). The ministers are tested by four kinds of allurement, and then employed by result:
- "those whose character has been tested under religious allurements shall be employed in civil and criminal courts";
- those tested under monetary allurements go to the revenue collector's and chamberlain's work;
- those tested under love-allurements superintend the pleasure-grounds;
- those tested under fear go to immediate service;
- "those whose character has been tested under all kinds of allurements shall be employed as prime ministers";
- those "proved impure" are sent to mines, forests and manufactories.

The text attributes the rule to earlier teachers: "in accordance with ascertained purity, the king shall employ in corresponding works".

**Why it is tested now.** AR-1 tested the Arthaśāstra's rule for reports. This is its rule for appointments, a different mechanism: it assigns by tested competence on a kind of work, not by agreement.

**The mapping (mine).**
- **The ministers** are the three members of the pariṣad (P-1, P-2): R-ORD+ANU+AKH, naive Bayes and the decision tree. Every member sees every label.
- **The tests** are each member's answers on earlier cases.
- **A kind of work** is the neighbourhood of the present case: the 10 earlier cases most like it. Likeness is the number of equal feature values; ties go to the more recent case.
- **UPADHA (employment by ascertained purity).** The case is decided by the member with the most right answers on those 10 cases. A tie between members is settled by plurality among the tied members' answers, with ties going to the earliest member.
- **GLOBAL-BEST ("tested under all kinds of allurements", the prime minister).** The case is decided by the member with the most right answers on all earlier cases, with the same tie rule.

## 2. Arms

| Arm | Who decides a case |
| --- | --- |
| PLURALITY | the three by plurality (P-1's assembly) |
| **UPADHA** | the member proved best on the 10 most similar earlier cases |
| GLOBAL-BEST | the member proved best on all earlier cases |
| UPADHA-RANDOM | the member proved best on 10 random earlier cases (`random.Random(seed + 1010)`). A control for the likeness of the cases. |

The members' predictions do not depend on the arm, so one pass gives every arm. Case 0 is not scored.

## 4. Hypotheses and decision rules

The decision rules are as before. The CI is stratified by dataset.

| Hypothesis | Comparison (prequential accuracy) |
| --- | --- |
| **H1 (primary)** | UPADHA − PLURALITY |
| H2 | UPADHA − GLOBAL-BEST: employment by kind of work vs one prime minister for everything |
| H3 | UPADHA − UPADHA-RANDOM: does the likeness of the test cases matter? |
| Descriptive | per dataset; how often each member is chosen |

**INVALID if:**
- any run is non-deterministic or crashes;
- UPADHA always chooses the same member.

## 5. Predictions before any AR-2 code

| Hypothesis | Prediction | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | ≈ 0 to small positive; NO WIN or UNDETERMINED | Plurality of three is strong (P-1). Selection gains where members' strengths differ by region. |

| H3 | positive | Likeness is the point of the rule. |
