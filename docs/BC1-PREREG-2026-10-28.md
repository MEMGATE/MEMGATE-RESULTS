**Status: DRAFT, 28 October 2026, 22:20 IST. Not frozen.**

- The hypotheses, rules and worlds below were written before any BC-1 run.
- It freezes after the 1.5B dev trial (`runs/bc1-dev-1.5b/w9792-v5`) and before any main world is packed. After the dev trial only mechanical fixes may change; the hypotheses and margins do not.

## Why
The learner keeps old knowledge because it rehearses everything it has learned in every session. So its training per session grows with what it knows. In CCL-7's dev world, the learner trains:
- 485 rows of the stream in session 1;
- 1,932 rows in session 34;
- 44,082 rows over the 34 sessions.

The objection: "rehearsing everything each month grows with what it knows." BC-1 tests the same learner with a fixed budget a session.

## The system under test
BC-1's learner is built from the payload of a full-learner run, with one change (`cl/bc1.py pack FROM_RUN OUT_DIR`). In each session it trains:
- **Every row first learned in that session or in the five sessions before it.** New items are practised without a break while new.
- **Older rows, only as many as fit the budget.** It takes the rows trained longest ago first. Ties are broken in a fixed order, by each row's SHA-256.

**The budget.** It is the mean number of rows of the stream the full learner trains in its first six sessions: the learner's cost while it knows little.
- A session whose new and young rows alone exceed the budget trains them all and no older row.
- So the cost per session is bounded by the stream's own rate, never by how much the learner knows.

**Why five sessions.** In CCL-7's first 7B dev trial (the full learner), items missed at the first check after training were:
- 8.7% in the session they were learned;
- 2.1% five sessions later;
- 1.2% or less at every age from six sessions on.

From six sessions on, an item is treated as firm, and it is revisited within the budget.

**Everything else is the full run's, unchanged:**
- the kernel (copied byte for byte) and the anchors;
- the self-test (about 200 items a session) and the practice checks (about 450), which are already of a fixed size;
- the questions;
- the removal of corrected errors, which replays the bounded sessions.

Only the learner runs. The full run's own learner, on the same world, is what it is compared with.

**Dev world 9792 at 1.5B (v5 anchors):**
- budget: 533 rows;
- rows of the stream over 34 sessions: 18,759 instead of 44,082 (−57%);
- from session 20 on: 533 a session, instead of 1,537–1,934.

## Arms
- **BOUNDED:** the learner with the budget, the run under test.
- **FULL:** the same world's learner from the run BOUNDED was built from. The report checks that BOUNDED's payload was built from FULL's payload (SHA-256).
- **The untouched model:** each run's own FROZEN answers.

## Hypotheses
All are BOUNDED − FULL after the last session, the family's worlds pooled. Each uses a paired bootstrap over items within each world (`ccl1.boot`): 10,000 resamples, seed 12345, 95% percentile intervals.

The measures are CCL-4's (`ccl2.measures`, and CCL-4's kept offices). "Routed" means the system as it answers, with CCL-4's scope rule (`ccl3.to_adapter`). "Weights alone" means every question is sent to the trained weights.

| H | Measure | View | NON-INFERIOR when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | Current holders of the offices it was told about | routed | lower bound above −2 points |
| B2 | Offices learned by session 4 and unchanged since (CCL-4's H3 set) | routed | above −2 |
| B3 | Offices first reported by session 12 and unchanged since | routed | above −2 |
| B4 | Dated questions | routed | above −2 |
| B5 | What follows (both kinds) | routed | above −2 |
| B6 | Lies admitted (poison) | routed | upper bound below +1 point |
| B7 | GSM8K | weights alone | lower bound above −3 |
| B8 | MMLU | weights alone | above −2 |
| B9 | Never-streamed offices of the world's own half (the other half's are rehearsed by CCL-7's office anchors and neighbour rows; CCL-7's correction of 29 October) | weights alone | above −5 |

**Why B7–B9 use the weights alone.** The system sends general questions to the untouched model, so both arms answer them identically. Only the weights alone can show a difference. Their margins are CCL-4's for H8, H9 and H10.

**Why −2 points for B1–B5.** The comparison is with the same learner, so the margin is tighter than CCL-4's −5 against naive fine-tuning. It is the margin of CCL-6's H15.

**Judging, in each family:**
- Item 18 is PASSED when all of B1–B9 are NON-INFERIOR.
- It is FAILED when one interval lies wholly on the wrong side.
- It is NOT SHOWN otherwise.

**Secondary (reported with verdicts, deciding nothing):** B1–B5 for the weights alone, margin −2.

**The cost (reported).** The budget bounds the rows of the stream by construction. The report checks what the machine did, from each run's log:
- the rows trained a session, mean of the last ten sessions over the mean of the first ten (BOUNDED is expected near 1; FULL grows);
- the seconds a session over the last ten sessions.

## Validity
A world is valid if:
- both runs finished every session with the done line;
- both answered every question after the last session;
- the removal of corrected errors ran in the session the full run's did;
- the untouched model's answers agree between the two runs (reported).

A run stopped by the machine without output is rerun once, in a new folder. Worlds not run by 10 November are left out, and the analysis states how many it pools.
