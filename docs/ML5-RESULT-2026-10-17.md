## What this means

**The lesson gain of ML-1 and ML-2 does not carry over to small real datasets.**

- Model-written lessons beat experience on synthetic worlds twice: +3.84 and +2.58 pp. Here they cost 1.97 pp.
- The loss is concentrated in the three autism sets: −2.7 to −6.0 pp for each model. On cervical cancer and prognostic breast cancer, the lessons gave +0.2 to +1.2.
- **Why autism, probably.** The autism label is "more than 6 of the 10 screening answers positive", an m-of-n concept.
  - A lesson in the model's format is a conjunction of feature values. It cannot state "at least 7 of 10" without listing many conjunctions.
  - So the model's short rules mislead it.
  - Naive Bayes, which adds evidence, is best on all three autism sets (86–91%).

**The mixed pariṣad still works.**
- Putting the lesson-using model into an assembly with naive Bayes and the decision tree lifted it by +1.72 pp (WIN). This is the third time an assembly of different kinds has rescued its weaker member (ML-4 +5.67 pp).
- The model member adds nothing over the offline assembly (−0.05). On real data, the model is one more voice, not a better one.

**The models stayed below the plain lesson learner:** ORD-sol was −2.17 pp below R-ORD. On 100 noisy cases, language models reading meaningless symbols have no advantage.
