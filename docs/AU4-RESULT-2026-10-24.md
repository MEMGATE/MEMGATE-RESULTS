## What it shows
- **The third version does not depend on a perfect registry.** With the registry wrong 5% of the time, it covered 6.90 points more than matched truth discovery (7.02 at 10%), held an attacker value at some point for 0.01% of offices, and dismissed no honest source in any world.
- **Every method that trusts the registry takes in its errors.** Never-true values were held at some point for 11.28–12.32% of offices by the registry alone, both versions and matched truth discovery at 5%. The third version was not worse: 0.62 points fewer than the second (95% interval 0.33 to 0.91).
- **The price of the third version is coverage, not robustness.** It covered about 2.3 points less than the second at 5%, and 2.5 points less at 0: the audit spends part of the question budget. The registry's errors cost it 2.44 points of coverage, about what they cost the second version (2.59 points here).

## Limits
- Model-free: it counts what each rule would hold in memory, not what a model answers.
- Simulated sources, attackers and registry errors over real timelines; a registry wrong in step with the attackers is not tested.
- AU-3's seven white-box attackers were not run here; their test assumed a correct registry.
