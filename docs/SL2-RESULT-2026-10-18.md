## What this means

**Kiraṇa 6's point replicates, strongly.**
- Evidence belongs to its locus. Keeping lessons and nearest cases to the case's own locus beat the same rule over random groups, on fresh data (+12.27 pp).
- SL-1's WIN was +1.04.

**How much it matters depends on what the loci are.**
- **Patients (Parkinson's).** Each patient has a stable level of disease. Reasoning within the patient lifts accuracy from 66.9% to 90.9%. Naming the patient as an ordinary feature gets most of the way (86.6%).
- **Weather stations in one city (Seoul).** These share one weather. Restricting evidence to the station throws away the city's common evidence, and costs 5 pp. SL-1 found the same with Beijing's stations, and predicted it.
- **Two regions with fire indices (Algeria).** The indices already carry the signal, so the locus adds nothing.

So the primary hypothesis of SL-1 (beating the plain learner) depends on the mix of datasets. With patients in the pool it wins; with only shared-phenomenon loci it loses.

**For practice.** Keep evidence per site, camera or machine when the units differ in baseline. Pool it when they watch one shared phenomenon. Naming the unit as a feature is a safe middle (+6.10 here, +0.72 in SL-1).
