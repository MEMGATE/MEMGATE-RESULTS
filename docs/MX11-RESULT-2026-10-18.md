## What this means

**The alarm works as Arthaśāstra 1.12 suggests.**
- When the three sources frequently differ among themselves, stop believing the network.
- Three-way disagreement, checked against its own baseline (prakṛti–vikṛti), caught two broken sources in 16 of 20 runs, about 43 cases after the failure. It never fired before a failure.
- With it, wrong answers alone after a double failure were 0.84 per 100, well inside the bound.

**MX-10's UNSAFE verdict was at the edge.**
- On these new seeds the supervisor without the alarm gave 1.16 per 100, also inside the bound. In MX-10 it gave 2.00, with a CI from 1.12 to 3.09.
- The true rate without an alarm is about 1 to 2 per 100. The alarm brings it down by about a third.

**The cost comes from the alarm being permanent.**
- Once it fired, the network asked about every later case. One false alarm in a sound run, and two alarms after a single failure that dismissal would have handled, were enough for HARM by the strict rule. The point costs are small (−0.84, −1.49).
- **The practical form for a working system** is therefore an alarm that pauses answering and tells the owner, then resumes when the owner confirms the sources are working. That form is not tested here.
