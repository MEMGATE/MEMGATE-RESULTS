# TW-4 spec: does the trained skill of reading a dated memory line hold when retrieval puts other offices' lines next to it?

**Status:** spec, written 23 October 2026, about 06:20 IST, before any TW-4 question. Code: `cl/tw4.py`.

## Why
TW-2 and TW-3 asked with one memory line per question. A deployed memory returns what retrieval found: the asked office's line among others, often of the same kind of office.
- SC-1 and SC-3 measured collisions between look-alike lines for frontier models.
- TW-4 measures them for small open models, with and without the trained reading skill.
- It also asks whether training with neighbouring lines helps.

## Arms
One open model per run, the lab's LoRA loop on Kaggle's two T4 GPUs, TW-2's settings: batches of 8 in micro-batches of 4, 2 epochs.

| Arm | Training |
| --- | --- |
| FROZEN | none: the untouched model |
| READER1 | TW-2's adapter: the same 1,008 rows on 336 of RP-1's offices, one line per question |
| READERK | the same questions, dates and order, but each question's memory also holds K = 4 other training offices' lines of the same form (the same kind of office first), with the asked office's line at a seeded random place |

## Questions
- **All three arms** are asked TQ-1's offices (dev 50, main 300) about TQ-1's BEFORE and AFTER dates:
  - SINGLE: the asked office's dated line alone (TQ-1's GATED+ words);
  - NEIGH: the asked office's dated line with four other TQ-1 offices' dated lines of the same half (the same kind of office first), at a seeded random place.
- **Locality:** RP-3's locality questions, with no memory.
- **Scoring:** TQ-1's scorer. An answer that names neither of the asked office's holders is also checked against the neighbours' holders, as a collision (NEIGHBOUR). BOTH as in TW-1 to TW-3.

## Hypotheses (to be frozen after dev)
An office-level bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, seed 12345.

| Test | Comparison | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1 (primary)** | BOTH right under NEIGH, READER1 − FROZEN | WIN: interval above +1 pp |
| H2 | BOTH right under NEIGH, READERK − READER1 | NON-INFERIOR: lower bound above −2 pp |

**Descriptive:**
- SINGLE results;
- the cost of neighbours for each arm (NEIGH − SINGLE);
- collisions;
- locality.

## Models
The three families of TW-1 to TW-3: OLMo-2-7B (primary), Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-Nemotron-Nano-8B.

## Limits
- **Four neighbours,** chosen by kind of office rather than by a retriever's score.
- **One line form** (the layer's).
- **Office holders only.**

## Data
- **Kaggle copies:** the payload goes up as a private dataset, and the run is a private kernel.
- **Clean-up:** both are deleted after the fetch.

## Dev, first Qwen run (23 October 2026, 06:22–06:38 IST): out of GPU memory in READERK's training
- **What ran:** the untouched model and READER1 were asked; then READERK's training ran out of memory on one T4. Its five-line rows in micro-batches of 4 gave too large a loss tensor with Qwen's 152,000-token vocabulary.
- **What was kept:** `runs/tw4-dev-qwen7b-20261023-oom`, not scored.
- **What changed:** micro-batches of 2 (`MICRO` in `cl/tw4.py`). Batches stay at 8, so the optimizer steps and the effective batch are unchanged. OLMo-2's and Qwen's dev runs are pushed again with this code.
- **Nemotron-Nano's first dev run** (06:28–06:50, the earlier code): it also ran out of memory in READERK's training, after the untouched model and READER1 were asked. It is kept as `runs/tw4-dev-nemotron8b-20261023-oom` and pushed again with micro-batches of 2.

## Dev 1 (23 October 2026, 06:42–07:24 IST, OLMo-2-7B and Qwen2.5-7B, 50 offices; descriptive only)
`runs/tw4-dev-olmo7b-20261023`, `runs/tw4-dev-qwen7b-20261023`. No question went unanswered.

| Arm | OLMo-2: one line | OLMo-2: with neighbours | Qwen: one line | Qwen: with neighbours |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| FROZEN, both dates right | 36 | 34 | 62 | 46 |
| READER1 | 100 | 78 | 100 | 84 |
| READERK (always four neighbours in training) | 52 | 80 | 100 | 96 |

- **Neighbours cost the one-line reader 16–22 points,** but it stayed far above the untouched model (H1 +44 and +38). Answers that named a neighbour's holder were rare (0–6%).
- **Training always with four neighbours helped Qwen** (+12). On OLMo-2 it helped little (+2) and broke the one-line case: 48% of after-change questions named the previous holder.

**What changed:** READERK is replaced by READERMIX. Each training question's memory holds a seeded random number of neighbours, from 0 to 4, so the reader sees both cases.

**The candidate tests:**
- H1: READER1 − FROZEN with neighbours, WIN.
- H2: READERMIX − READER1 with neighbours, WIN.
- H3: READERMIX − READER1 with one line, NON-INFERIOR at −2.

Dev 2 runs this design on the three families.
