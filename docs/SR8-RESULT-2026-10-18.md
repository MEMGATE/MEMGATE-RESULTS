## What this means

**The Gītā's three kinds of knowledge (18.20–22) rank as the text says, on average.**
- Sāttvika knowledge, the one seen in the many (lessons that unify cases), beat rājasa knowledge, each case kept separately (the nearest case), by +1.71 pp.
- Rājasa beat tāmasa knowledge, clinging to one effect as if it were the whole (the most frequent label), by +15.93 pp.
- **Dataset by dataset, the order is not fixed:**
  - On very imbalanced data (kc3, SpeedDating), the single most frequent label beat both other learners.
  - On word counts (authorship) and on autos, the nearest case beat the lessons.
- So the text's ranking describes the usual order of these learners, not a law that holds everywhere.

**Saṃyama, a lesson formed on the spot from the case's own neighbourhood, adds a little.**
- +0.33 pp on held-out data, significant but below the margin, and +2.94 on noisy synthetic worlds.
- It does not beat a plain vote of the seven nearest cases (−0.74). Concentrating on the neighbourhood helps; forming a rule there adds nothing beyond counting its cases.

**The krama check, a text-preservation method, rejects true cases when applied to data.**
- Overlapping pairs of adjacent features that "break" because they have never been seen with the case's label set aside up to 20% of all cases (jasmine), and cost −3.93 pp.
- In a fixed text, a never-seen pair is an error. In data, a new combination is usually a genuine case: a rarer class, or a new pattern.
- It helped only a little on imbalanced two-class data (kc3 +1.7, SpeedDating +1.3), where a rejected case is more often noise.
- It also failed on the stable synthetic worlds: EXC −7.09, UPA −10.08.
