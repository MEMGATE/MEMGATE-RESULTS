## Rules fixed in the code

**SAMYOGA.**
- When lesson formation reaches a pure conjunction within one kind, the literal of another kind that keeps the most covered cases is added. The earliest feature breaks a tie, and this one literal may exceed the usual three.
- If the case has no value of another kind, the formation fails.

**RANDOM-KINDS.** The run's kind labels are permuted by `random.Random(seed + 888)`, so every kind keeps its size.

**INVALID conditions:**
- any non-determinism in the repeats;
- a failed run;
- SAMYOGA never joining a second kind.

## Dev run (exposed data)

The output is in [`runs/rs1-dev-20261018-a`](../runs/rs1-dev-20261018-a/SUMMARY.md). It covers O-2's six datasets with documented groups, in orders 2000–2009. There were 60 runs, all 6 repeats were identical, and nothing failed.

| Comparison | pp, 95% CI | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1: SAMYOGA − R-ORD | −0.11 [−0.26, +0.04] | NO WIN |
| H2: SAMYOGA − RANDOM-KINDS | −0.06 [−0.22, +0.10] | NO WIN |

**Findings.**
- SAMYOGA joined a second kind hundreds of times per dataset and never failed to form.
- Yet the lessons' accuracy hardly changed, with the documented kinds or random ones.
- Pure conjunctions in this learner are mostly decided by their first literals. The added literal narrows coverage a little and changes few decisions.

No bug was found, and nothing changed after the dev run.
