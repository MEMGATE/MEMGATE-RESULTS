**The methods compared:**

- **ENGINE:** the full engine (gate, self-check, scope rule, rollback and replay).

## What this means

**Neither the most stable published approach nor rehearsal asks where a report came from.**
- On this stream, merging learned the changes (96.3%), but also 17.6% of the poison, and the swarm lies' trace in nearly every target (97.5%).
- It left 15.2% of untouched offices answered correctly, barely better than naive training (12.9%).

**The gate is a plug-in.**
- **What it fixed.** Put in front of merging, and nothing else changed, it took poison to 0 and the swarm lies' trace to near chance (52.0%). The method also learned a little more (99.0% current).
- **What it did not fix.** The gate decides what enters; it does not decide where an update lands. Merging into the shared weights still rewrote untouched knowledge (15.8%), and it cost general retention (87.1%).

**The scope rule and the self-check are what protect everything else.** Only the full engine kept untouched knowledge (98.5%), general retention (100%) and its own competence suite (99.6%) at the frozen model's level. It also learned the most changes of any arm (98.7%).

## Limits, stated plainly

- **Our implementation of LoRA merging,** with α chosen on dev as the spec required. Other merging schemes (weight averaging over checkpoints, task arithmetic with tuned scales) and knowledge editors (MEMIT, AlphaEdit, WISE) were not run.
- **SE-5's world.** Real offices, simulated sources, 177 facts per world, twelve sessions.
- **One model.**
