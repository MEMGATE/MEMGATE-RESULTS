## What each mechanism did

**ANU (anupalabdhi: a missing value is observed absence).** This is the first mechanism in the whole SR programme with a significantly positive effect.

| Dataset | ANU − R-ORD (pp) | 95% CI |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Soybean | +1.61 | [+1.44, +1.80] |
| Primary tumor | +1.07 | [+0.36, +1.78] |
| Audiology | −0.31 | [−0.71, +0.13] |
| The four datasets without missing values | 0 | |

In soybean and tumor, *which* values were left unrecorded carries information. That is exactly the anupalabdhi claim: non-perception of what would have been perceived is knowledge of absence.

**VIN (liṅga before experience).** VIN helps on splice (+0.79) and hurts on audiology (−2.04), tumor (−1.07) and soybean (−0.87). A lesson that is one condition short of matching is a weaker guide than the nearest case. That fits the sūtra's own ordering, in which each ground is weaker than the one before. It does not show that liṅga beats experience.

**PAN (para: the later lesson decides).** The worst loss is MONK-1 (−4.25). Recency beats support only when the world changes, and these data do not change.

Rajpopat (2023) reads 1.4.2's *para* as the rule that operates on the right-hand operand, not the later rule. Our "later lesson" is the traditional reading carried over to time, and it is a stretch.

**BAD (identical past case first).** In a precision-1 learner no active lesson can contradict a stored case. 1-NN already returns the most recent identical case, so BAD changes nothing.

**Mixes.**

- MIX-ORD inherits the losses of VIN and PAN. On MONK-1 it loses −4.80.
- MIX-SH keeps SR-2's advantage under noise (NOISE +1.57) and its weaknesses (DRIFT −4.10).

## Conclusion

Of the rule mechanisms tested so far, only anupalabdhi gives a measurable, significant gain on real data, and the gain is small. It applies only where absence is recorded.

Viniyoga ordering and the Pāṇinian para order, in these readings, make an ordinary learner worse. Pratyakṣa precedence is already implicit in it.
