## Rules fixed in the code

**Images.**
- An event's images are the identity, all η negated, all φ negated, and both. The η columns are lepton and jets 1–4; the φ columns are lepton, missing energy and jets 1–4.
- Images are mirrored on the raw values and binned with the original events' H-1 cut points.
- A KOLAM learner observes each distinct image after the case itself.

**INVALID conditions:**
- any non-determinism in the repeats;
- a failed run;
- a KOLAM arm that observes no image.

## Dev run (the dev part, rows 20,001–30,000)

The output is in [`runs/k3-dev-20261018-a`](../runs/k3-dev-20261018-a/SUMMARY.md): 10 orders (2000–2009), 60 runs, all 6 repeats identical, nothing failed.

| Comparison | pp, 95% CI | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1: R-ORD+KOLAM − R-ORD | +0.78 [−0.28, +1.85] | UNDETERMINED |
| H2: EXT-1NN+KOLAM − EXT-1NN | −0.44 [−1.34, +0.50] | NO WIN |
| H3: EXT-NB+KOLAM − EXT-NB | +1.36 [+0.56, +2.13] | WIN |

**Findings.**
- **The binning is nearly symmetric.** 98.6% of flipped angle values land in the mirrored quartile (q0 ↔ q3, q1 ↔ q2), and every case had 3 distinct images.
- **Naive Bayes gains most** (+1.36), against my prediction (≈ 0). Two effects are mixed:
  - The images make each class's angle distributions symmetric.
  - They also count every unchanged feature four times, which weakens naive Bayes' add-one smoothing.

  The design cannot separate the two.
- **1-NN does not gain.** The nearest neighbour of a case is often one of its own earlier images' neighbours, which adds nothing new.

No bug was found, and nothing changed after the dev run.
