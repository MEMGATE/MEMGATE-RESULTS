## What it shows
- **The result holds on a second library, and is larger.** On polars, the gated library raised passes over the poisoned one by 72.78 points (pandas, SK-1: 43.33).
- **The strongest models were not safe this time.** gpt-6-astra and gpt-6.1-sol, which ignored nearly every attacker recipe on pandas (0 and 2 of 30 programs), followed them on polars in 27 of 30 programs and passed 3. Only claude-opus-5-5 mostly resisted (4 of 30). Both migrated every program correctly with no library at all, so they knew the right way; a confident wrong recipe in their own skill library still overrode it.
- **Without any library,** three models could already migrate every program; the other three passed 0–53%. The gated library brought those three to 83–100%.
- **What GATED did not fix:** gpt-4.1-base and llama-4-maverick failed 5 of 30 programs each. Nine of those ten failures applied `approx_n_unique` to every column, as the recipe's example `df.select(pl.all().approx_n_unique())` reads, instead of to the one column the program selected, and polars refuses it on the list column; the tenth called a namespace as a function.
- **The gate,** unchanged from SK-1, admitted all 28 true recipes and none of the 28 attackers' before the run: every attacker recipe used a name polars 2.0.0 does not have.

## Limits
- One kind of skill (API migration) on two libraries; short programs built from three changes each.
- The attackers' recipes are invented names (CD-1's fabrications); a recipe that misuses real names would pass this gate's check and is not tested.
- The models may have seen some of polars' changes in training; NONE shows how much they knew on their own.
