## Rules fixed in the code

**Members.** PARISHAD-3*: R-ORD+ANU+AKH, naive Bayes and the decision tree. All three see every label.

**UPADHA.**
- The 10 earlier cases most like the present one are chosen. Likeness is the number of equal, present feature values; ties go to the more recent case.
- The member with the most right answers on those cases decides.
- Tied members are settled by plurality among their answers, ties going to the earliest member.

**GLOBAL-BEST.** The same, using all earlier cases.

**UPADHA-RANDOM.** The same, using 10 random earlier cases drawn from `random.Random(seed + 1010)`.

**INVALID conditions:**
- non-determinism;
- a crashed run;
- UPADHA always choosing the same member.

## Dev run a (exposed data)

It is in [`runs/ar2-dev-20261018-a`](../runs/ar2-dev-20261018-a/SUMMARY.md):

- 215 runs in 234 s.

| Comparison | Real datasets (pp) | Synthetic families (pp) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1: UPADHA − PLURALITY | −0.36 [−0.51, −0.22] | −0.39 |
| H2: UPADHA − GLOBAL-BEST | +0.10 [−0.06, +0.26] | −0.17 |
| H3: UPADHA − UPADHA-RANDOM | +0.81 [+0.64, +0.99] | +0.56 |

**Findings.**
- **Likeness matters.** Members proved on the most similar cases choose better than members proved on random cases.
- **Choosing one member does not beat the three voting together.** The members tie on the 10 cases in 35–85% of cases, and plurality then decides anyway.
