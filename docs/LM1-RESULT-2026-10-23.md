## What it shows
- **The dated memory adds to retrieval.** For both models, adding the layer's notes of every session to the retrieved rounds raised accuracy by 11 and 18 points. The gain was largest where retrieval misses the spread of facts: on multi-session questions, from 50.5% to 78.4% and from 52.6% to 90.7%.
- **It matches or beats the whole history, with a third of the tokens.** GPT-4.1 did 9.35 points better with the layer's memory than with the whole history in context. gpt-6.1-sol, which reads long contexts well, did as well with it (94.9% against 94.6%).
- **Time questions, against the whole history:** GPT-4.1 gained 5.62 points, an interval that includes zero; gpt-6.1-sol was level. The prediction of a win for GPT-4.1, from dev, did not hold.
- **The memory's notes and the rounds work together.** By the code scorer, the notes alone or the rounds alone were each below the two together.

## The hand labels and the code scorer
- **Agreement:** the code scorer matched the hand labels on 95.0% (GPT-4.1) and 93.5% (gpt-6.1-sol) of answers. Every verdict is the same under both.
- **Consistency:** of 1,488 answers that appeared word for word more than once, only one group (3 answers) got different labels. Settling it by majority leaves every verdict unchanged.

## Limits
- **Not comparable with published numbers.** LongMemEval's own scoring uses GPT-4o as a judge; these are the lab's blind hand labels.
- **The notes are made by GPT-4.1,** so their errors are part of what is measured, and the same notes served gpt-6.1-sol.
- **BM25 is a simple retriever.** A stronger retriever would raise RETRIEVAL and, likely, LAYER.
- **What is tested here is the memory's form:** facts with their dates, the later replacing the earlier. The layer's gate, scopes and undo are not tested, since LongMemEval has one trusted user and no attacker.
- **Two models,** one release of the benchmark.
