## The question

RM-2 to RM-4 showed that one line in a raw memory's own format, claiming to be the registry, steers frontier models into the lie. That memory put each report's text on its own line as it came, so an attacker's text could start a line of its own.

A careful engineer would escape untrusted text before it enters a prompt. RM-5 asks two things:
- whether two standard ways of doing so stop the attack;
- whether the value gate adds anything once they are in place.

## What this means

**The vulnerability is in how the memory is written, not in the model.**
- When untrusted text can start a line of its own, a forged line looks like the memory's own provenance, and the model believes it.
- When every report's words are quoted on one line, or visibly marked as data, the same model ignored every payload: 0 of 314 facts.
- That includes the spoof and the instruction, which steered it on about 60% of facts alongside forged lines in the raw memory.

**Practice for memory that holds untrusted text:**
- never concatenate it unescaped;
- keep provenance in a field the source cannot write;
- tell the model that the source's words are data.

These are standard prompt-injection defenses, and here they were enough.

**What the gate adds.** It matched both defenses on poison and truth. Beyond that:
- **Prompt size.** Its prompts are 15–19 times smaller: 42 tokens against 639 and 787.
- **It does not depend on the model.** It does not need the model to read a format or follow an instruction. A weaker model, or a stronger attacker, cannot talk it out of what it has not admitted.
- **It answers questions the format cannot.** Which of two disagreeing sources is right, whether a value is current, and whether a pair of colluders is enough to believe them. In RM-1, a raw provenance-tagged memory was poisoned on 16.6% of attacked facts after four sessions and left 27.6% of changed facts stale under a looser instruction; the gate was at 0 and 100% throughout.

**RM-2 to RM-4 read with this.** Their finding stands as measured: a raw memory that concatenates untrusted text unescaped can be steered by one forged line, across frontier models. RM-5 shows the cheapest fix for that channel, and where the gate is still needed.

## Limits, stated plainly

- **One model:** `gpt-6.1-sol` at its default effort. Weaker models may follow quoted or marked payloads more often.
- **The payloads were not adapted to the defenses.** An attacker who knows the escaping scheme may find text that still persuades. RM-5 does not test that, and this lab does not develop such attacks.
- **The world is synthetic and the attacks scripted,** as in RM-1 to RM-4.
