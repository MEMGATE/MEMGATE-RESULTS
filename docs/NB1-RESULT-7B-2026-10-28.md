## What ran

- **5e-6**, the rate cited for plain sequential fine-tuning.

**Setup:**
- Model: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in 16 bits.

**Validity:** all four runs are valid. NAIVE finished its 12 sessions in each run (exit code 0, the done line) and answered every question of the evaluations after sessions 4, 8 and 12. Lie scores exist for every run.

## Reading
**A small learning rate keeps general ability.**
- At 2e-5 and 5e-6, GSM8K stayed at 92.0–94.0, against 37.3 and 70.0 at CCL-4's rate.
- MMLU stayed at 71.5–74.5.
- So the point that plain fine-tuning with a small rate forgets less general ability holds here.

**It does not keep the facts it was not trained on.**
- Offices never in the stream were answered right 12.5–21.3% of the time at the small rates.
- That is no better than at CCL-4's rate (21.7 and 29.3), against 99.2% for the untouched model and the learner.
- New facts of the same kind overwrite the old ones whatever the rate.

**At 2e-5 it still takes in lies.**
- It named the attacker's value for 3.3% and 10.9% of attacked offices; the learner named none (N1 WIN).

**At 5e-6 it learns too little.**
- It named the current holder of 70–71% of changed offices; the learner named 100%.
- On dated questions it fell below the untouched model (6.6 and 12.3, against 17.0).
- It also took in few lies (1.1% and 2.2%), so N1 is not shown there.

**Questions on what changed are answered well only by the learner.**
- Dated questions and what follows, at both rates: +33 to +57 points for the learner.

**MMLU is not shown at either rate.**
- The learner keeps MMLU exactly, and so does naive fine-tuning at a small rate.
- The intervals' lower ends sit at −2.00 and −4.00 against a margin of −2.
