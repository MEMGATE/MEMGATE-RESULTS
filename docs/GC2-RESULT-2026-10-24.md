# GC-2 result: multi-step math (GSM8K) after the dated-line reading skill is trained into three open 7–8B models

**Written:** 24 October 2026, 17:39 IST, after all three families were fetched (Nemotron-Nano last, at about 17:35). Preregistration: [GC2-PREREG-2026-10-24.md](GC2-PREREG-2026-10-24.md) (frozen 12:39 after two dev runs). Code `46da32ec…`.
**Runs:** `runs/gc2-main-{qwen7b,olmo7b,nemotron8b}-20261024`, Kaggle's two T4s; each trained TW-2's reader again (TW-2's code, unchanged) and asked both arms the whole GSM8K test split, 1,319 questions, zero-shot, at most 512 new tokens. Machine summaries: each run's `report.json`, from `python cl/gc2.py report OUT_DIR`.

## H1: READER − FROZEN, non-inferiority at −2.0 points (paired bootstrap 95% interval)

| Family | FROZEN | READER | READER − FROZEN [95% CI] | Verdict |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct | 91.66% | 90.75% | −0.91 [−2.05, +0.30] | **NOT SHOWN** (by 0.05 points) |
| OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct | 82.87% | 78.09% | −4.78 [−6.90, −2.73] | **NOT SHOWN** |
| Llama-3.1-Nemotron-Nano-8B-v1 | 60.65% | 35.78% | −24.87 [−27.82, −21.83] | **NOT SHOWN** |

**In words:** after the reading skill was trained in, non-inferiority on multi-step math was shown for none of the three families. Qwen2.5-7B stayed within a point (its interval's lower end missed the margin by 0.05 points); OLMo-2-7B lost 4.78 points; Nemotron-Nano lost 24.87 points. Every question got a reply with a number, in both arms of every family.

- **Nemotron-Nano stopped working through the problems.** After the training, more than half of its replies were a bare "Answer: N" with no steps, against 15% before; the reader's 1,008 training rows answer with a name only (median 14 characters), and this model carried that habit over to math.
- **OLMo-2** kept writing steps but lost accuracy; its reader's last training batch ended at a loss of 0.32 here (Qwen's 0.0001, Nemotron's 0.03), so its training went less cleanly.
- **Qwen2.5-7B** was barely moved, as on MMLU (GC-1: −0.79).

## Reading, with GC-1
- On general knowledge (MMLU, GC-1), the skill cost two of the three families nothing measurable. On multi-step math it costs something for all three, a little for Qwen and a lot for Nemotron-Nano.
- The cost comes from the training, not from the layer's rules: the reader is a small LoRA trained on short answers, and a short-answer habit reaches tasks that need steps. A lab would mix the reading data with general data, or keep the dated line in memory instead of training a reader (the route most of our results use); this run tested neither.
- The brief and report say the reading skill is not free on every task.

## What this does not show
- Two benchmarks in all (with GC-1), zero-shot; three small open models; the reader's training only. Facts trained into the weights (TM-1's route) are not covered.
- The reader was trained again for this test; Kaggle's T4s do not give bit-identical training, so it is TW-2's recipe, not TW-2's adapter.
