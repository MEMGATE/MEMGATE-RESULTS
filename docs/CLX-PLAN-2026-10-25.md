# CLX: complete continual learning, from small tests to large ones (plan of 25 October 2026)

**Written:** 25 October 2026, 10:17 IST, after the founder stopped every test and asked for a fresh start: complete continual learning from the same principles as before (other ideas added where those fall short), small tests first, then larger models and larger tests.

## Where things stand (preregistered results)
- **CCL-2, Qwen2.5-7B: one learner, all twelve abilities at once** ([result](CCL2-RESULT-2026-10-25.md)).
  - Passed: 2 (no lies), 5 (dates), 6 (skills), 7 (reasoning with new knowledge).
  - Failed: 3 (never-streamed knowledge, in the weights alone).
  - Not shown: 1 (new facts, 3 points below naive), 4 and 9 (GSM8K −3).
- **FT-4, Llama-3.3-70B, one step** ([result](FT4-RESULT-2026-10-25.md)): every prediction held.
  - No lies.
  - What follows: 88–96% against 21–23%, with transfer above the untouched model.
  - Math kept.
  - Never-streamed knowledge fell for every trained arm.
- **The scope rule** (learned knowledge applies only to what was learned; descriptive) keeps never-streamed knowledge and general ability. CCL-3, its preregistered test, was stopped before a result.

## The ladder
| Step | Model and world | Machine | One test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Qwen2.5-1.5B, a small world (56 offices, 30 never-streamed) | Kaggle, two T4s | under an hour, every arm |
| 2 | Qwen2.5-7B, full worlds; dev, preregistration, main | Kaggle | about 2.5 hours per world |
| 3 | GPT-4.1, -mini, -nano (session by session) and Llama-3.3-70B (one step) | Azure hosted fine-tuning | about 12 hours per chain |
| 4 | Qwen2.5-72B | Kaggle's TPU (started from the website) | queue, then about 4 hours |

Small tests are exploratory: they decide what earns a preregistered test at step 2 and above. They are never reported as results.

## The gaps, and what is tried first
1. **Never-streamed knowledge, in the weights.**
   - First: reciting the untouched model's own answers about many offices it knows. These offices are never streamed and never tested, and the recitation happens every session at scale. A first version with 100 offices raised locality only from 18% to 25% (SE-3).
   - Then: the scope rule inside the weights, with an adapter per area of knowledge, active only for questions about it.
   - Then: added ideas, such as keeping the trained model's answers on other inputs close to the untouched model's.
2. **Learning better over time.** The learner sets its own practice per kind of item from its self-test, and the test measures whether acquisition gets faster across sessions.
3. **New facts against naive training.** State the change explicitly ("Y now holds it; X no longer does") as well as the dates, so the past does not pull answers about the present.
4. **Removing what was learned wrongly.** A stream where the learner first believes some errors and later learns they are wrong, so the measure has room to show a difference.
5. **General ability, in the weights.** More of the untouched model's own answers to general prompts.

## First small test
`cl/clx.py`, world 9790 (dev), Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. Three arms:
- NAIVE;
- LEARNER (CCL-2's learner);
- LEARNER+RECITE: the learner plus 400 recitation rows a session (200 offices the 7B knew in the pool probe, from both pools, never in this world, wordings 0 and 1).

Read at session 12: never-streamed offices for each arm, against the untouched model; every other measure, to see what the recitation costs.

## Third small test (CLX-3): the scope rule learned inside the model
`cl/clx.py --wide`, world 9790 (dev), Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. Two arms:
- NAIVE;
- LEARNER+GATE: CCL-2's learner, plus a gate learned from the model's own hidden states (the last prompt token, with the adapter switched off). At each evaluation the gate, a logistic regression, is trained on the prompts of everything the learner has trained on so far (positive; not the general prompts or the rule rows) and on 300 office questions about offices outside this world plus the 64 general prompts (negative). A question goes to the adapter only if the gate gives it more than 0.5; every other question goes to the untouched model. Three gates are fitted on the same rows, one per kind of feature (all from the untouched weights): the last prompt token's state at the final layer, the same token at the middle layer, and the mean middle-layer state over the question's own words; each is read on its own.

Never-streamed offices: every office the 7B knew in the pool probe that this world does not use, 303 instead of 30, so that differences of a few points can be read. The gate never trains on them.

Read at session 12:
- LEARNER+GATE as the gate routes it, against NAIVE and the untouched model, on every measure;
- the gate against the exact scope: questions about learned offices and skills to the adapter, all others to the untouched model;
- the same arm without the gate, which is the learner alone on the wide set.

## Fourth small test (CLX-4): the model tells for itself what it was taught; removing errors
`cl/clx.py --wide --errors 8`, world 9790 (dev), Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. Two arms:
- NAIVE;
- LEARNER+ASK: CCL-2's learner, and the model itself learns to say whether a question is about something it was taught. Every session it also trains on up to 120 of the questions it trained on that session ("yes") and as many office questions about offices outside the world and general prompts ("no"). At each evaluation it is asked this for every question, and a question goes to the adapter only if it says yes. Models fine-tuned as a service (GPT-4.1) give no hidden states, so this is the form of the scope rule they can have.

The registry also reports a wrong holder for 8 offices for three sessions and then the right one, so both arms first learn errors and later their corrections (ability 4, removing what was learned wrongly).

Read at session 12: LEARNER+ASK as it routes itself, against NAIVE and the untouched model, on every measure; its routing against the exact scope per kind of question; how often each arm still names the corrected wrong holders.

## Fifth small test (CLX-5): the whole system in one
Arms NAIVE and SYSTEM, and SYSTEM+GATE if the gate does better than the model's own routing in CLX-3b and CLX-4. SYSTEM is CCL-2's learner with:
- **practice until firm** after each session's training: it is asked every office it holds (wordings 0 and 1) and every skill it rehearses, and trains again only on what it missed (two rows each, with eight of its general-prompt rows), up to three rounds;
- **the scope rule learned by the model**: routing by asking (SYSTEM) or the gate on hidden states (SYSTEM+GATE);
- the world of CLX-4: 303 never-streamed offices and 8 registry errors.

Read at session 12: every measure of the routed system against NAIVE and the untouched model; the routing per kind of question; how often the corrected errors are still named; and, for learning better over time, the share of each session's newly learned items missed at the first check after training, which should fall across sessions.

## The TPU route (no waiting for the GPU quota)
`cl/clx_tpu.py` runs the same arms on Kaggle's TPU v5e-8 with the JAX model (`cl/jax_qwen.py`; Qwen2.5 1.5B to 72B, the 72B in 8 bits), with the same payload and the same report. The gate on hidden states runs on the GPUs only. Kaggle's API gives this account CPU machines for TPU requests, so each run is saved as a notebook and the account holder starts it from Kaggle's website; one TPU run at a time. First on the TPU: the whole system on Qwen2.5-7B in CCL-2's whole dev world 9790 (`--share 1`: 177 offices, 117 of them changing; 263 never-streamed offices), with 8 registry errors.

## Sixth small test (CLX-6): learning better over time
CLX-5 settled the system for open models: CCL-2's learner, practice until firm and the gate on hidden states (with known offices among its negatives); the model's own routing rows did harm and left it. CLX-5 did not show learning better over time: the share of each session's new items missed at the first check after training did not fall. CLX-6 adds the learner's control of its own practice (SYSTEM+GATE+ADAPT): when more than a tenth of a session's new items are missed at that first check, the next session repeats its new items once more (up to four times); when none are missed, once less. Arms NAIVE and SYSTEM+GATE+ADAPT; the world of CLX-5 (303 never-streamed offices, 8 registry errors). Read: the first-check miss rate of new items by session (should fall), the repetition the learner chose, and every other measure against CLX-5's SYSTEM+GATE.
