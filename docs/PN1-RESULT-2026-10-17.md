## What this means

**Naming a contiguous stretch of an ordered inventory with one literal is a real gain for a rule learner.** This is the device of Aṣṭādhyāyī 1.1.71, *ādir antyena sahetā*.

- With range literals, one lesson condition can say "q1 to q3" instead of needing several lessons, one per bin.
- On datasets where the label depends on a feature *crossing a level* (grid stability, Coimbra, Avila), the lesson learner gained 2–4.5 pp.
- The gain was preregistered as the primary hypothesis and passed the full rule. It also replicated the dev run (+0.58 pp on 16 exposed datasets).

**The specifically Pāṇinian part, an inner stretch that touches neither end, adds nothing** (H2, −0.02 pp). End-anchored stretches, which is what ordinary threshold literals ("≤ q1", "≥ q2") give, carry the whole gain. The honest conclusion is that the *idea* of naming a stretch helps, and ordinary thresholds already capture it. The tradition's device is a correct and useful representation, but not one that beats the standard engineering analogue.

**Other learners.** Naive Bayes is hurt (−2.2 pp), because overlapping stretches count the same evidence several times. ID3 never chooses a coarser split of a feature it can split four ways.
