## How to read these results

**H1 and H2 are not evidence that nature follows the pañcāṅga.**

- The matching worlds were generated from vāra, pakṣa, nakṣatra and māsa cycles. A representation that contains those exact periods is bound to fit them.
- What T-1 does establish is narrower: the pañcāṅga is a compact, learnable multi-cycle code. Generic dyadic phases (mod 2 to mod 32) cannot express 7-, 27.3- or 29.5-day periods, so they gain nothing.
- Where the hidden cycle is not a pañcāṅga period, the code neither helps nor hurts on average. It costs 1.6 pp on the 10-day cycle and gains 1.9 pp on the 45-day cycle through partial alignment.

**H4 is the first WIN for a mechanism inspired by the Kālacakra.** The mechanism is comparative: it comes from the Buddhist tradition.

- When no lesson decides a case, the learner searches recent experience first (the last 30 cases), then the middle layer (cases 31–180 back), then older cases. It accepts the first layer whose best match is within one feature of the best match overall.
- This helps where rules change:
  - DRIFT-T: +3.66 pp.
  - SEASON: +4.81 pp, because the seasonal regime shifts.
- It is neutral elsewhere.
- Computationally this is recency-biased retrieval, a known idea in ML. The Kālacakra supplied the inner-recent, outer-old layering that Ember's KC-EXP-1 could not test because of its budget design.
