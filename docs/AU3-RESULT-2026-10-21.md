## What this means
- **A determined white-box attacker did get through gates v1 and v2.** The attacker:
  - earns trust with true reports;
  - spends the gate's question budget on decoy disputes;
  - plants fabricated values in pairs on offices the registry never reports unasked.

  This put fabricated values into the gate's memory, for a session or two, on up to 7.4% of offices. Slower variants got 1.5–1.7% through in every world.
- **Gate v3's two added rules closed those routes:** random audits of values about to enter memory, and exposure at entry.
  - Worst case 0.18% against 4.12%, the best of any arm that learns from testimony.
  - Coverage within 0.35 points of v2.
  - No honest source dismissed.
- **The strongest remaining attack is sparse_early,** written against v3: three fakes per pair per session hidden among honest values. It still places a few values for one session: 0.18% of offices, at most 0.45%.
- **Against the standard methods:**
  - Strict truth discovery (TD-0.98) has a worst case of 0.57% at 2.4 points less coverage.
  - Majority vote and lenient truth discovery let 25% and 12% through.
  - Using the registry alone lets nothing through but covers 14 points less.

## What this does not show
- **Seven attackers of our own.** v3 was built after the first three and its audit share set after the fourth, on dev worlds. These main worlds test it on fresh data, not against new attackers. A new design may do better.
- **No outside red team,** by the founder's decision: the gate's rules are not shown to any outside party.
- **The registry is always right here.** v3 has not been tested with a noisy registry (AU-2 did that for v2).
- **Simulated sources and attackers; model-free.** The 20-account, 2,000-report limit is this test's choice.
- **"Beaten" counts a fabricated value held at the end of any session,** even if it is caught a session later. In the dev trace of patient_decoy against v2, the fabricated values were gone two sessions after they entered.
