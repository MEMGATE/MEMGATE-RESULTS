## What this means

**Verification against the owner's answers identifies broken sources reliably.** Every one of the 21 spies it dismissed was broken; it never dismissed a working one. A broken source cannot fake this signal, unlike agreement (MX-12).

**Wrong answers still slip through, at two points.**
1. **Before the alarm.** It needs about 40 cases of excess three-way disagreement. Meanwhile, two broken spies sometimes join a wrong report of the working spy.
2. **After a partial dismissal.** In 30 paused cases, only one of the two broken spies often falls clearly below the working one. The network then resumes with one broken spy still trusted.

**MX-10 to MX-13 together: the two-failure case.**

| Experiment | Supervisor alone (no alarm) | Best alarm variant |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| MX-10 | 2.00 | — |
| MX-11 | 1.16 | permanent 0.84 |
| MX-12 | 1.88 | permanent 1.12 |
| MX-13 | 2.12 | permanent 1.38 |

Values are wrong answers alone per 100 after the failure.
- **The rate.** With two of three sources broken, the supervisor gives about 1–2 per 100, against about 0.6 when sound. The fixed bound of 2 sits inside the seed-to-seed spread, so verdicts flip between experiments.
- **The permanent alarm** cuts the rate by about a third every time, at a cost when it fires falsely.
- **The two resuming alarms** keep credence (NO HARM when sound) but give back most of the safety.

**For a working system.**
- When the sources start disagreeing three ways, do not wait for statistics alone. Raise the alarm to the owner at once.
- Keep the system paused until the owner confirms which sources work, using verification against the owner's answers, which here was never wrong.
- Use longer verification than 30 cases before resuming.

A design with those two changes would need its own test.
